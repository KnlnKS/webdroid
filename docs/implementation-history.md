## Evidence and measurement

### Noise, host load and false regressions

- **Host load manufactures regressions** (2026-09-30..10-05). Parallel suites and live runs multiplied TikTok wedges and mid-transition settles, on the starting commit too. A TikTok "regression" (8/23 vs 1/17 wedges) was 3/8 vs 3/8 interleaved; Komikku's step after "Get started" failed 2 of 14 parallel runs against 0 of 24 before a logging change, with matching step times. doodlejump `05 back` times out ~1 run in 5 at any commit under load; a single-run bisect blamed an unrelated commit.
- **Noise floors** (2026-10-01..05). At load 30–58 (five agents, 14 cores) every latency number was noise, and samples taken while another checkout runs browser tests establish nothing. On an idle M4 Pro, 10–30 ms gated-tap differences are noise. jbench within one commit: b64java 1.00–1.13, mix 1.34–1.45 ms.
- **Live content is a variable** (2026-10-03). YouTube's intermittent video-open failure was a sponsored card (up to 1,600 px) under the fixed tap at y=1350; relay and QUIC were exonerated (matching packet counts, same failure with UDP blocked). Logging every datagram slowed boot past the 18 s bound.
- **Ticks are not frame rates** (2026-10-02). A Mihon sample that missed its setup target reported advancing ticks with no presentations. Perf windows that are hidden, stalled, input-incomplete, looper-unresponsive or present nothing are marked invalid, and missing setup targets fail.
- **Passing controls are not causal closure** (2026-10-05..07). For FitNotes' `RenderNode` crash, three timing-perturbing changes passed 39/39 without catching the stale handle; only a registration-generation trace found it (see [Rendering and text](#rendering-and-text)). Observer and Promise instrumentation shift scheduling, so they can expose or hide races.
- **Contaminated range** (2026-09-30). `git commit -am` swept a probe tracing every main-queue job into `28fd6e26`; e2e timings up to `19534e7c` are inflated.

### `lat` and response observation

- **Pixel observer cost; off-thread sampling rejected** (2026-10-08/09). The whole-image `sig` (draw, downsample, synchronous readback on the main thread) took 98.3 ms self in a 300 ms FitNotes tap window. A Worker prototype made responses 714–810 ms: analyzed frames lag the latest presentation during animation, so the join accepts only after animation ends, and accepting older pixels would weaken the gate. GPU-backed synchronous sampling kept all 117 nonuniform predicates but only nine exact hashes (max mean-channel diff 0.00464). Neither integrated.
- **Retained-frame readback** (2026-10-09/10). Under SwiftShader even a cropped readback, `VideoFrame.copyTo` or a fenced WebGL PBO read blocked up to 264 ms, so `lat` crops candidate frames on the GPU as they present and reads them back once presentation pauses. Taps then presented ~20 frames instead of 2–4; earlier tap numbers are not comparable.
- **What a tap time means** (2026-10-07..10). Scenarios observe the intended semantic response and owned app pixels (`06bfe74e`); a first changed bitmap is a proxy. The console's per-tap compiler figure stopped at the first changed frame (1.8 ms) where the intended response had 15.4 ms compile and 10.9 ms module work. Independently rounded child/root clocks gave false ordering (`e4cb5239`). A dialog dismissal presents no app frame, so `lat` times it against the next unrelated one (Komikku "OK": 2.5–2.9 s, state matched in 33 ms).
- **Compile accounting overlaps** (2026-10-07..09). Compiler spans (`b668afe3`) exclude main linker/planning and module parsing, and elapsed time is not thread CPU. Compile, module and sampled figures overlap; compilation counters include recompiles. Per-step `perfReset` erases cumulative counters: zero root AOT loads in a step does not mean none loaded.
- **`lat` launch conditions** (2026-10-10/12). `lat` boots from `?apk=` at load, so gains measured from `wd.boot()` on a pre-loaded page (spare zygote, warm) do not show in it.
- **Harness-only costs** (2026-09-27..10-03). Playwright's `Runtime.enable` makes `new Error()` capture detailed stacks (~20 vs ~4 µs): Snapseed's ~1,140 reflective `NoSuchFieldException`s per tool open cost ~65 ms only under the harness.
- **Scroll window limits** (2026-10-08/09). Presentations/s include static intervals and are not dropped-frame counts. Swiping every 500 ms instead of 1,000 ms cut idle time. ABBA pairs drift with run order.
- **Private drivers drift** (2026-10-05..09). One passed untranslated Flow actions and sent no input; a scroll driver and corpus each dropped a query setting and silently ran another configuration. Drivers share `executionFlow`/`actOf` and `webdroidUrl()`.
- **Throttling reaches only the page** (2026-10-13). Chrome refuses `Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate` on a Worker session ("Operation is only supported for pages, not workers"), and a page's rate does not slow its Workers. In a busy-loop probe the Worker loop took 1,081 ms at rate 1 and 1,097 ms at rate 4, while the page loop took 177 and 728 ms. `lat --throttle` therefore slows only the page thread, and figures "at 4× throttle" for app processes in Workers measure unthrottled app code. The refused call also made `--throttle` with `--cpuprofile` drop every Worker profile. `lat --ecores` replaced it (below).
- **A slower device on the efficiency cores** (2026-10-14). macOS `taskpolicy -c background` confines a process and its children to the efficiency cores at low priority, which slows every thread alike: the busy-loop probe went 169 → 1,044 ms in the page and in Workers. `lat --ecores` launches Chrome that way. Cold `--gpu` runs: Calculator M launched in 15.6 s and its first taps (`7`, `plus`) presented no response within the window, then `equals` 94, `clear` 184 and `8` 79 ms; FitNotes launched in 10.3 s with taps of 39–827 ms (`start` slowest) and scrolled at 16.5 presentations/s (p95 101 ms); Komikku launched in 49.4 s, `next` 828, `more` 1,189 and `settings` 705 ms, `start` and `appearance` presented nothing in the window, and scrolling reached 16.4 presentations/s. The floor on a slow device is the cold first tier, not the frame path.
- **One filesystem's space, in bionic's layouts** (2026-10-14). Native statfs/statvfs reported a fixed 4 GiB filesystem with 2 GiB free while Java's StatFs reported the browser estimate (a fresh Chrome profile gets a 10 GiB quota; a TikTok launch uses about 215 MB). Both now read the estimate. The old fills were also mislaid: ARM32 statfs is the kernel's statfs64, with 64-bit counts from offset 8, and the 4-byte writes put `f_bfree` in `f_blocks`' high word; A64 statvfs is eleven 8-byte longs, written as 4-byte fields. `stp` translates its value as a guest pointer, so stat times and other C longs stored with it could become a high plane's address on A64; those now use `stLong`. TikTok's intermittent "Manage your storage" dialog (two of three warm preparations on one day, then none in five matched runs, with server-delivered text) was not caused by native statfs (cause below).
- **TikTok's "Manage your storage" was a failed StatFs** (2026-10-14). Its check (`CleanCacheDialog`, run when `last_check_storage_time` allows) shows the dialog when `new StatFs(getExternalCacheDir().getPath())` reports under `diskFreeSpaceThreshold` (440 MB unless the server sets `disk_space_threshold`), and StatFs failing counts as -1 bytes. `Os.statvfs` and `setxattr` looked a path up only in the calling process's VFS, while an app's private and external app directories belong to another realm, so `statvfs` threw ENOENT for `/data/data/<pkg>`, its `files` and `/sdcard/Android/data/<pkg>/cache`. They now ask the owning realm as `stat` did. The check runs once per fresh profile, when the app first reaches its dialog manager, which is why it looked intermittent. In matched `e2e --full --test tiktok` runs (alternating, fresh profiles), both runs without the fix showed the dialog at step 03 after `X.06b6.LJIIJJI`'s StatFs got ENOENT; both with it ran all 13 steps without it, `statvfs` answering. With StatFs answering, a cold TikTok launch also stores `files/ugcommon` resources it skipped before. Free space itself was never short: headless Chrome reports a quota of usage plus ~10 GB.
- **Batched MOVEs (`?input=batched`)** (2026-10-13, 5 alternating runs per mode, page throttled 4×, app unthrottled). Komikku `scroll-appearance`: 59.9 vs 59.2 fps, p99 24.8 vs 33.6 ms, frames over 25 ms 2 vs 4 (medians). FitNotes `scroll-legs`: 59.4 vs 60.0 fps, 1 vs 0 over 25 ms, with `doFrame` p95 1.2 vs 0.8 ms. Input-to-dispatch rose from 0.1 to ~3.5 ms by design. This is no clear win: immediate dispatch stays, and the batched path was removed (cbd04094; restore it from 116c1c53).
- **First response frame** (2026-10-13). `lat` now gates on the first presented frame after `pointerup` that changes a tile (8 px) the settled frame also changed, outside the tapped node's bounds plus 72 px and outside tiles that changed while pressed; the settled state is reported beside it. The Worker sends ≤128 px crops of the response regions with each presentation, and its timeline ring (`src/timeline.ts`, read with `wd.foregroundTimeline`) attributes each window: vsyncs, doFrame, draw, composite, present and loop idle/wake. `--throttle N` applies CDP CPU throttling to the page and every Worker; `bundles=0` keeps bundled app builds out of cold runs. The fitnotes scroll started at y=1690, outside the exercise dialog (about 522–1617), so its 41 fps measured drags that scrolled nothing; the Chest list it now drags fits on screen (3 presentations/s), so that step does not measure pacing either.
- **Crashed pages and dead app processes** (2026-10-13). A warm YouTube run waited forever on an app that had died in `handleBindApplication`, and a crashed renderer (ChatGPT warm, one TikTok cold launch, the page and its Workers sharing one renderer) left `Runtime.evaluate` pending. `lat` now listens for `Inspector.targetCrashed`, bounds the partial read and the close at 10 s, and fails a launch once the app's process has a crash record (`wd.workerProcesses()`). A scenario's launch `ms` widens the launch window (ChatGPT and TikTok need 60 s cold). The renderer crashes are not yet explained; memory is the likely cause.
- **Komikku `next` races the dialog's aftermath** (2026-10-14). On the profiled build `next` failed 3 of 8 warm runs at 180–256 ms, the base commit included. In a failing run no press ripple drew after `pointerdown`, the looper already held a message about 690 ms out, and after `pointerup` no Java ran for 186 ms before the next screen drew; passing runs had nothing scheduled. With 3 s instead of 1.5 s after "OK", 6 of 6 passed (22–36 ms). A later series (2026-10-14, 3 of 7 warm runs slow at 125–240 ms) split cleanly: fast runs drew a frame for every vsync after DOWN (the press ripple), slow ones ticked `doFrame` each vsync (0.2–1 ms) without drawing from DOWN until the response. A probe on `nSyncAndDrawFrame` showed the app never asked to draw in that gap (first call at +188 ms, the response), and no thread ran Java (190 of 240 ms with none). Input arrived with `uptimeMillis` event times and was handled in 1 ms, and vsync carries `uptimeMillis` as its frame time. The app waits on its own clock, as a delayed animation would; the cause inside the app is not identified and the scenario is unchanged.

### e2e capture, settling and determinism

- **One guest clock**. Animation, draw and scroller time read `performance.now` while `ValueAnimator`/`SystemClock` read guest time; unifying on `uptimeMillis` made FitNotes runs identical. The controlled clock still advances to due timers while `drive` waits on real work, so virtual time per step depends on host speed (CalculatorM's ripple varied up to 17/255).
- **Controlled-clock traps** (2026-10-03..12). A real 60 ms tap hold passed the 400 ms long-press deadline on a controlled clock; UP now follows DOWN by 60 guest ms. Delayed messages (AbsListView's click 64 ms after UP) never ran unless the clock kept running through the pixel sampling window. Pinning `Date.now` froze `currentTimeMillis` and splash conditions; guest wall clock is start plus guest time. A settle that advanced the clock once left a later Calculator ripple requesting a frame every vsync (3 of 5 runs timed out); the probe now advances 64 ms per sample while a vsync is scheduled, up to 2 s. Non-live flows run offline because Mihon's update dialog covered every screen.
- **Synthetic swipe timestamps** (2026-10-05, `daf308dd`). Delayed timers stamped a synthetic swipe's catch-up positions as if the finger accelerated, so Compose flung Mihon's settings list and a checkpoint intermittently missed; `src/main.ts` now passes the scheduled sample uptime to `src/shell/device.ts`. A forced 100 ms stall reproduced it; one passing baseline had not.
- **Pixel settling pitfalls** (2026-10-04..07). An overscroll stretch draws no new pixels, so a settle there let a tap land in the scroll. Repeated `-1` sentinel hashes once passed the stability rule, reporting `pixels` with zero presentations; the probe now requests a redraw until a sample exists and accepts only a nonnegative stable hash.
- **Mirrored frames queued behind a held-up page** (2026-10-15). A process Worker mirrored every presented frame to the probe as a full-screen ImageBitmap (~10.5 MB at 1080x2424). With the page thread blocked for 20 s, as an 18 s readback blocked it in a failing CalculatorM step, 622 copies queued; that is the renderer's growth per redraw before "Target crashed". The page now acknowledges each mirrored frame, and past three untaken the latest frame waits: 20 copies across the same stall, and the full ~30/s once the page is free.
- **A booting process is work under way** (2026-10-15). A settle that ended while CalculatorM's GMS process (pid 1003) was still loading packages failed the launch step's diagnostics; frames spinning at a frozen clock had kept such settles presenting long enough to hide it. The probe now waits while the broker reports a Worker that has not served its endpoint.
- **Probe readbacks starved the broker** (2026-10-12). Under SwiftShader a full-screen `getImageData` waits for the GPU process's backlog (0.3–1.1 s per Komikku sample, up to 6.5 s), and the broker answers app IPC on the page thread, so the app Worker idled. Readback beyond 150 ms is now limited to a fifth of the page thread: Komikku `09 manga` 7–21 s → 1.7–1.9 s; Calculator's pixel-settled steps slowed to 2–4 s.
- **Stable pixels are not success** (2026-10-05). A FitNotes pass used a coordinate fallback and ended on a spinner; a later failure reproduced on control: home was still loading when Start New Workout fell back to its point, so taps landed one dialog behind. Required text actions now wait for their target, coordinate typing for an enabled editable target, and virtual horizons let a fling end before the next input (`AbsListView.onTouchDown` ends a fling instead of selecting). Either fix alone failed.
- **Flows must require observable state** (2026-10-07). Reddit's One Tap sheet sometimes arrived after launch, so an unconditional Back closed Reddit; live waits can now require a state. TikTok's `liveSample(90_000)` was cut by a 50,000 ms outer bound; the watchdog now adds a live wait's dwell.
- **Reading process diagnostics** (2026-10-05). Capture checks each Worker's copied snapshot (`worker:<pid>`) against the broker row's PID, process and package, so a misrouted one fails. Root-only draw counters cannot see a displayed Worker; `wd.displayCanvases()` lists every shown surface. Aborted captures once lost all steps and reported zero; completed steps are now kept.
- **Capture pitfalls** (2026-10-08). Page-level Playwright routing missed service-worker fetches, so a "candidate" Resy run executed the original bundle: patch at context level and verify with runtime markers. Resy's API uses the TCP/TLS relay, so an empty HTTP observer is not an empty API result. `locateText` searches the combined window tree, so a hit does not prove the view under a dialog got the tap.
- **Mihon launch checkpoint is black** (2026-10-09). The launch wait admits any three presentations, so the PNG is black while the UI tree holds the library; launch visual parity is unproven. Warm FitNotes runs lack PID 1002 at the pre-input snapshot, so diagnostics cover post-tap paths only.

### Profiling and attribution

- **Profiler pitfalls** (2026-10-03..09). A V8 CPU profile's first sample carries the gap since `Profiler.start` (`(program)` inflated to 15%); a forced full GC before a window deoptimized ~360 functions; inspector start (20–89 ms) admits extra compilation and does not align with HR time, so profiles show ancestry, not warm-window percentages.
- **Multi-target attribution** (2026-10-05/07). Bracket profiler start in every target with clock markers, stop all concurrently and clip to the common window: sequential start/stop made the app crossing delta (55,559) exceed the step's own (39,326). Keep harness work (`uiautomator.walkView`, page pixel hashing) separate: FitNotes' conservative boundary bound was 96.57%, largely the harness's `getImageData`. `WebAssembly.Module.imports` omits enabled js-string builtins; count from binary sections.
- **Measure in V8, not Bun**. JSC ran a loop 52% slower where V8 was unchanged and names no Wasm frames, so caller-class lookups fail; since 2026-10-12 `bun run test` runs each `*.test.ts` in its own node process. Node launchers must use `bundleForNode` and stay alive with a `setInterval` (the yield channel is unref'd); `--cpu-prof` writes nothing without an absolute `--cpu-prof-dir` and a process not ended by `process.exit`.
- **Stalls the timeline cannot see** (2026-10-13). Three readings of the same Komikku stall were wrong before a trace with V8's GC categories named it: a hidden process skipping vsync (its `Visible` marks never changed), a lost yield-channel wake (the main strand resumed only after an unrelated compile-worker message), and a slow page (a `fileAccess` answer took 84 ms). The Worker was in one engine task all along. A silent stretch with neither rAF nor the fallback timer means the thread itself was held: check GC and compile tasks in a trace before suspecting the scheduler. `lat --json` taps now carry the page's own timeline and long tasks (`page`), and the Worker marks served calls (`Serve`/`Served`) and visibility changes.
- **Idle waits and memory**. Bucketing a `lat --cpuprofile` by 100 ms (`scripts/lat/profsum.ts`) found the serial zygote phase, which now overlaps the APK download. A CDP heap snapshot aggregated by retainer path found per-realm CanvasKit, jar and framework-res copies; `performance.memory.usedJSHeapSize` omits Wasm memory. In OOM messages "8 MiB" is `allocSlow`'s heap-check window and "512 MiB free" is fixed text.

### Probes, differentials and debug hooks

- **Differential probes need large seeds**. API-33 probes found 0/1,000 differences in seven API recipes and 0/100 graphics differences on two small seeds, but 1,000-case graphics seeds had 58 and 74 (matrix/clip, glyph coverage, Porter-Duff edges).
- **Lifecycle differential through EventLog** (2026-10-04). Diffing each step's `wm_on_*_called` tags (30021-30065: event, activity, reason) from the AVD's `logcat -b events` against the page's `[I/EventLog]` found a never-sent TopResumedActivityChangeItem, a no-op `recreate` and a splash finish race. An AVD replay that diverges from the flow is not evidence.
- **Device and headless probes**. Build with d8 (`androidTool("d8")`), run `CLASSPATH=/data/local/tmp/x.dex app_process / Main` after `ActivityThread.systemMain()` (`su 2000` for shell-uid answers) and diff against the probe run headless; charset tables and UTF-32 behaviour came from this. Headless, only `zygoteInit()` (after `rt.linkNatives()`) routes `System.out` to logcat; ICU needs `mountPlatformData`, Typeface `loadSystemFonts`. `textcmp` waits for the bound application, not `bootMs`.
- **Debug hooks**. Set before boot (`page.addInitScript`): `__wdNativeTrace`/`__wdNativeStep`, `__wdWatch` (registers at pcs), `__wdSlowMs`, `__wdGlProbe` (`getError` after each GLES call). `wd.threads()` snapshots strands by Java thread with wait state and owner (`wasm:<strand>`) without calling Java; it shows owner cycles, not lock objects.
- **Reading a failed run**. `newErrors` is de-duplicated, so temporary `console.error` probes show only in `browserErrors`. A blank Flutter surface says nothing alone: diff the page logcat between two paths (Lichess).

### Benchmarks, sweeps and conformance

- **Kotlin-shaped workloads against ART** (2026-10-14, plan 0.6). G1 gained nine `kt` cases (final getter chains, a lone interface implementation, generic lambdas, parameter null checks, IntArray slot tables, field updaters, try/finally, a continuation state machine, stackless cancellation), and `scripts/wasm/g1art.ts` runs the same cut methods with `app_process` on the reference AVD. Steady state in Chrome against ART: getter chains 9.3×, field updaters 7.5×, null checks 5.2×, slot tables 4.8×, the lone implementation 4.4×, lambdas 2.4×, the state machine 2.3×, cancellation 1.6×, try/finally 0.5×; geometric mean 3.2×. Catches are not where the gap is (plan 3.5's typed catches). Unsafe already compiles to a per-field accessor, and an updater's offset is a runtime value, so constant-offset Unsafe (3.5's other half) would not reach updaters. For plan 3.6, a node `--trace-wasm-inlining` run of `ktGetters` shows V8 inlining all three getters (and a small helper per call) into the loop's second-tier copy, so same-module direct calls would add nothing; what remains per iteration is the casts, guards and loop fuel around the inlined loads. Skipping every cast (V8's unsafe `--experimental-wasm-assume-ref-cast-succeeds`) bounds what precise reference types could remove: node steady state 1.61× on the getter chain, 1.0–1.26× on the rest (1.17× geomean), and skipping null checks as well added nothing. Warm Komikku taps were unchanged with every cast skipped (sums of tap medians 333 vs 330 ms over three alternating `--gpu` pairs, scroll p95 17.2 vs 17.3 ms), so precise types have no tap to shorten. Plan 3.4's `acmp` on typed operands is two of those casts in an inlined helper. Its per-literal String globals do not fit the module builder: a defined global's index follows the imported ones, and units import globals (literals, statics) while they compile, so each later import would move it; a literal load stays a table read in a function V8 inlines. The AVD's ART runs on the same host, virtualized.
- **G1 (`scripts/wasm/g1.ts`) must use the production loader layout** (2026-10-08). The harness flattened platform and app DEX into one Program, making library classes application-owned (production: `new Program(appDexes, platform)`); flattened results do not stand for production. The fix moved ArrayList steady 0.126→0.083 ms (Node) but linkedList 0.036→0.271 ms. G1 fails on incomplete samples or input drift; first-call time includes async entry creation. Shipping AOT keeps the suspending import ABI, so harnesses using it need promising entries.
- **Sweeps validate, not execute** (2026-10-05/07). TikTok's sweep exhausted V8's ~4 GiB Wasm code space because the ordinary World instantiated runtime helpers for discarded units; validating in the compiler Worker's `compileOnly` World (`cacae7a3`, `06230811`) cut peak RSS 13.8 GB (Bun) → 4.35 GB (V8). A zero-method report (split XAPKs once read only top-level DEX) never passes.
- **Conformance runs need a frozen tree** (2026-09-30). The runner spawns a child per test class that imports `src/`, so edits mid-run contaminate it: run from a detached worktree at a committed snapshot, and do not start two suites at once in a fresh worktree (the first builds sql.js).
- **Conformance false failures and accounting** (2026-09-30..10-07). Test names embedding `Instant.now()` or the date (`TCKInstant`, ojluni-time's TestZoneTextPrinterParser) report "passing tests no longer ran" yet pass alone; TestZoneTextPrinterParser (86 s alone) exceeds the 240 s class timeout beside other suites. A class setup failure losing baseline-passing names fails coverage even if both tiers lose them. The runner adapts TestNG 7.4's array providers to Android's `GenericArrayTypeImpl` without changing bytecode. `OutputStreamTesterTest`/`WriterTesterTest` hide private `suite()` methods: a known 124-case gap. An empty result buffer does not mean nothing ran: Guava Maps hit 600 s after ≥237,568 tests because suite construction took 445 s.

### Test-runner and tooling traps

- **Slow or misleading tests** (2026-10-02..12). BigInteger `shiftRight(Integer.MIN_VALUE)` takes 8 s: AOSP allocates a 256 MB magnitude per operand. `customdiff` takes ART's answers for byte-array-view atomics, which JDK 22+ rejects.
- **Waits that do not wait** (2026-10-05/06). Playwright `waitForFunction` returns at once for an async predicate: poll from the host. Bun's `rejects.toThrow` hung on port-transported rejections. Bare-path `bun test` picked up ignored recovery-checkout copies: pass `./tests`. `corpus` exits 0 with a crashed row.
- **Fixture bundle corruption** (2026-10-02..08). Intermittent browser SyntaxErrors under concurrent runs were corrupted Bun HTTP bodies of the ~7.97 MB fixture bundle (a repeated 65,536-byte block, backward shifts, a truncated tail); the fixture now materializes the build once before serving nine pages. Bun's trigger is unknown; other one-off failures did not reproduce. The same corruption still reaches the platform jars, which are served from memory: about 1 full `bun run test` in 6 fails 1–9 `app.browser` tests with `ZipException: End Of Central Directory signature not found` (2026-10-14). An instrumented failure had framework.jar (43,302,820 bytes) and a 372,077-byte jar each arrive at full length, with mid-archive bytes at the tail. Isolated stress against Bun and against 12 headless Chromium pages, with and without prior range reads, returned 0/48 bad copies, so only the loaded in-process fixture server shows it.
- **Thread-ordering fixtures** (2026-10-05). A queued-monitor order `20,1,…,19` came from a fixture recording invocation, not acquisition, order. Independent host entries on one Java main thread are same-thread class-init re-entries (IntegerCache seen half-initialized); use real `Thread.start/join`.
- **Proxy and server traps** (2026-10-08..13). A timing proxy must keep HEAD headers (a dropped content-length broke APK ZIP loading) and forward WebSockets, or timings describe offline runs. A long-running dev server keeps the old imported format version, so after a bump the AOT manifest 404s and runs lack persisted code. The platform key kept naming `oracle.ts` after its rename, so for a stretch of commits pages compiled the platform on demand.

## Compiler

### Symbolic sites

- **First-execution resolution** (2026-10-07, `e722bae5`). App symbolic references resolve at first execution through the initiating Java loader; a compiled opcode adapter replaces the initial table thunk. Batching sites before their owners resolved produced 43 modules; batches take only resolved owners and never extend a sealed batch.
- **Sites emit from operand metadata** (2026-10-09). Site metadata gives each input's DEX register and ABI kind; after loader resolution the linker decodes only the executed instruction and emits the field/call/cast operation, instead of preparing and typing the whole method per site. Warm FitNotes exercise list 239.2/215.5 → 193.5/192.5 ms; calendar taps mixed.
- **Bounded reuse of operations** (2026-10-09). Operand identity (aliases, ABI, opcode, reference, caller class) is nearly emitted-code identity (of Calculator's 43,550, 461 repeats differed only by an `init` import omitted after initialization). The World keeps 2,048 recent functions under that key; a hit installs one without compiling (except links with invalidation dependencies or module-owned state). Calculator first-digit modules 1,993 → 1,447; latency mixed. Since the static default namespace, symbolic sites barely occur (Mihon 18), so rewriting each site in place does not pay.
- **Stable helper names** (2026-10-08). Resolved helpers are named `symbolic:operation` (no `MethodInfo`; stack recovery skips them), so identical bytes reuse engine code with no runtime cache. 12,000 helpers: 695.79 → 352.29 ms, RSS growth 481.5 → 116.5 MB.
- **Rejected: helper batching and caches** (2026-10-08). In TikTok, 11,446 of 45,073 eligible helpers were byte-exact duplicates. Not integrated: whole-method batching (more heap, blank YouTube home); 4-op basic-block batching (FitNotes instances 32,738 → 31,550, heap +0.6 MB, no compile gain); inline resolution via shared imports and a mutable slot (no compile gain, Resy final heap 1,059 vs 844 MB); an exact-export cache (instances 16,220 → 15,473, keys retained 1.4–5.8 MB, no app gain). Reuse needs a sound identity boundary and measured retained memory.

### Loops, chunks and stack depth

- **Outlined loop chunks** (2026-10-05..06, `9abe6438`). Loops and dispatch cycles re-enter outlined functions with typed activation state and preserved fuel, so long activations reach optimized code; code grew ~17%. Each irreducible/handler cycle is one chunk. Tier 2 keeps loops inline unless one ran 64 chunks in one entry (`World.longLoops`): outlining them copied state per entry and shrank V8's inlining budget (inline: Node steady state 1.25× → 1.73× of the former JS tier). Rejected: moving an inline tier-2 loop into a chunk at a fuel tick (large methods stopped inlining), direct calls to final targets (neutral), callee-sized compile estimates (worse steady state), a JSPI yield fast path (a suspend costs ~55 ns).
- **Irreducible cycles** (2026-10-05). Rare (8 of 219,874 framework methods). Region strong components include handler edges from instructions not marked `NO_THROW`; in cyclic ones, non-forward edge targets are outlined so every cross-region cycle re-enters a compiled function.
- **Fuel on back edges** (2026-09-29..10-05). Fuel covers every back edge, including arithmetic loops, mutable bounds and overflowing increments (a finite-loop exemption was unsafe). A rotated loop's back edge can target a higher pc, so checks go on retreating edges, not `target <= pc`. A compiler stack overflow no longer installs a permanent missing-method body.
- **Chunk depth and frames** (2026-10-05). Entry reserved the largest nested chunk stack for the whole activation (Mihon SOE in Compose layout); a chunk charges dispatcher+chunk only while active, restoring caller depth on return and throw. Chunks declared the whole method's locals per recursion level (Komikku SOE → SIGKILL in recursive `reportGroup`); logical locals get compact native indices, while state struct fields keep method indices.
- **Physical local reuse** (2026-10-05). ChatGPT hit `$throw_soe` at 912 Wasm frames: MC allocated one Wasm local per web, and depth accounting charges physical frame size. `allocateWebs()` shares storage across disjoint conservative liveness intervals of one physical type: ChatGPT `ug0.invoke` 329 → 79 locals; skipping it reproduced SOE at 917 frames. Rejected: inflating Java depth.

### Calls, guards and CHA

- **Hot-call guard selection** (2026-10-08). Over its two-guard limit the planner dropped all guards, so hot iterators or `Integer.hashCode` among five `hashCode` targets were never copied. It now filters by the 64-execution copy threshold and inlinability, then keeps the top two. Steady listIter 0.158 → 0.084 ms (Node), linkedList 0.230 → 0.090 (Chrome).
- **Planner work** (2026-10-07..08, `be97a845`). Planning decodes prepared instructions once (845 → 169), skips the implementation-set walk while the allocated-class set has not grown (148,500 → 14,850 classes) and shares one CHA answer between direct and guarded planning (678 → 340 queries); work counts, not time. CHA reachability walks the owner's subtree filtered by receiver, not the receiver's subtree per owner.
- **CHA assumptions in `install` builds** (2026-10-10). In platform and app builds a CHA target never changed the emitted call (guards cover only same-module targets), yet app overrides such as `ArrayList.size()I` invalidated 3,623 bodies per warm Mihon launch. No longer recorded (3 invalidations; Mihon first Dark tap 427–435 → 89–97 ms); final classes/methods record none (`eb7f97e8`). Limit: ART's link-time rejection of subclassing final classes or overriding final methods is not enforced.
- **Late overrides** (2026-10-07). A late interface implementation did not invalidate a slot assumed after symbolic dispatch; prepared identities now carry guards the override invalidates. Raw helpers decline unowned CHA devirtualization; absence-dependent helpers own negative slots and rebuild when classes arrive.
- **Rejected: direct calls in precompiled code** (2026-10-11). Same-module direct calls crashed Mihon: a host-implemented method (`HostClasses.overlay`) keeps its bytecode body, which direct callers reached. Without host classes, results matched an unchanged rebuild within noise; hot code already gets V8 inlining via second-tier copies.
- **CHA only for targets the module holds** (2026-10-14). A single CHA target changes emitted code only through `callGuards`, as a guarded direct call to a method of the same module, and the guard compares slots at run time. `Compiler.devirt` now answers "many" unless the module is direct and defines a method of the call's name and descriptor. This skips the query and the assumption, and the invalidation on class arrival, for one-method first-tier units and for merged and precompiled code; codegen is unchanged. A node compiler benchmark (one unit per Komikku method, as on-demand compiling makes them) went from 138 to 129 µs per method. g1 first calls were 1.05–1.2× faster. Steady state fell ~2% (`tryCall` 0.096 → 0.134 ms): identical first-tier code upgraded later, past `longLoops`' 64 chunks, so the second tier outlined its loop. Matched cold Komikku taps were unchanged (sums 588 vs 584 ms), and launch compiling fell 2,837 → 2,712 ms. Rejected on the way: no CHA at all (steady 0.76–0.82×), and CHA only in the second tier (steady 0.91–0.97×), since first-tier batches hold callees too.
- **Compiler front-end throughput** (2026-10-14, plan 2.5). The same benchmark (an even sample of Komikku's methods, 3,000 per rep) went from 129 to ~108 µs per method; every step kept the compiled output byte-identical (`a65229da…`). `Flow` builds each region in one pass with shared sort scratch (160 → 122 ms); `typeConstants` follows only registers a float read or a receiver reaches, copying block state on write (452 → 404 ms); inference keeps operand nodes in one `Int32Array` (440 → 384 ms); `planDepth` and the facts pass test opcodes, not names or maps of every instruction. A call's function type is memoized by descriptor, and `DexFile.methodTypes` answers by proto, so no descriptor is parsed or hashed per call. What remains is spread thin: module encoding (~13%, mostly copying `number[]` bodies into the output) and helper bodies. `Array.prototype.concat` of a body (which carries `functionRefs`) made `finish` and `build` 2.5× slower.
- **Rejected: final targets called through their slot** (2026-10-13, plan 3.2). Of Komikku's ~230k virtual sites, 154k name a final class, 34k a final method and 35k a single CHA target (a static census of its call sites). Calling a final target as invoke-direct does sped g1 steady state 1.05× (Chrome) to 1.07× (Node); String rows gained 1.3–1.8×. Matched Komikku `lat` runs (3 per side, alternating) were unchanged warm and within noise cold. In 2 of 4 warm launches with the change, the app was presenting a frame when the `next` tap's DOWN arrived, and the whole response region moved while pressed, so the response clock failed. This happened in 0 of 10 warm launches without the change, and in 0 of 3 with it limited to final classes; the cause is not established. The platform build is compiled without host classes, and specs also register at runtime (`rt.registered`). A spec that implements an inherited method of a final class (`HttpsHandler.openConnection`, from `HttpHandler`) is reached only through the vtable repair (`vtableRepairs`), which a slot call bypasses. Final targets record no CHA assumption, so nothing invalidates such a call. Revisit only with host-aware invalidation and an app-level gain.
- **Not pursued: inlining trivial bodies** (2026-10-14, plan 3.3). By bytecode shape, field gets and puts, static gets, constants, argument returns and empty methods received 5–7% of the calls a cold Komikku launch counted in each process (iget 4.7–4.8%; `AbstractStringBuilder.length`, `Integer.hashCode`, `Object.<init>` lead). Hot ones reach the second tier, which the engine inlines. Saving a call sequence on the rest is a few milliseconds over a launch.
- **`Math` transcendentals as host imports** (2026-10-13). As natives each call was counted, caught and Promise-checked; the intrinsic imports host `Math` (Komikku tap 112k → 15k crossings, latency unchanged). `StrictMath` keeps fdlibm. Intrinsics shadow natives, so natives under intrinsic keys were pruned as unreachable.

### Front end and register typing

- **Floating constants and handler typing** (2026-10-04). Numeric DEX constants need reaching definitions across CFG and exception edges to tell floats from null. In `prepared.ts` an int constant assigned after the last throwing instruction was typed float in the handler, so `l >>>= 32` shifted by 0.
- **Nullable `invoke-custom/range` argument** (2026-10-07). Null-check analysis treated it as an instance invoke and marked argument 0 non-null, so an array access skipped Java's null check and trapped outside the NPE handler (present at `5f45a4ea`); the classifier uses the instance-invoke predicate.
- **Catch resolution** (2026-10-07). Catch types resolve through their Java loader only for the active try range, in order, keeping the original throwable when one fails to resolve (ART `FindCatchBlock`). Only catch-all-only methods dispatch directly (`94491b6a`).
- **VarHandle arity** (`2f92c766`). A VarHandle call with fewer parameters than required values is rejected before storage emission; the bridge throws `WrongMethodTypeException`.

### Binary emission and module size

- **Unused imports pruned** (2026-10-08..09). Modules declared every host function; encoding drops unreferenced ones. Single-method sweep bytes −28% (batched platform AOT ~0.2%); helper heap per instance 6,042 → 1,642 B. Only surviving import signatures are interned, per build: 2,000 field helpers 418 → 250 B each, build+instantiate+run 45.9–53.4 → 27.7–33.8 ms; no reliable FitNotes change.
- **`ModuleBuilder` encoding** (2026-10-08). Writes ASCII names in place into section-level arrays: 737 captured builds ×30, 153.34 → 82.36 ms Node, 259.65 → 79.66 ms Chrome. Relocating in the final copy was dropped to keep relocatable bodies apart from custom-section bytes.
- **Prologue helpers** (2026-10-11). Wasm/DEX size is 5.72×. The inline call counter (~34 B) and stack check became per-module `count`/`enter` calls: module code −15%, launch lazy compile only 259 → 245 ms. Binaryen `-O1` shrinks app module code 17.5% (a small compile-time lever).
- **Class-global import metadata** (2026-10-07). WebKit 26.5 runs modules importing typed GC globals, but `WebAssembly.Module.imports` throws on them; unit headers list required class-global imports.

## Linker and runtime

### Class loaders, resolution and rebinding

- **Static default namespace** (2026-10-10, `64ed4a59`). ART resolves a `PathClassLoader` with the boot parent natively, so app DEX references link at compile time like boot code, not as per-site modules (`loaders.ts` `standardPath`); other graphs call `World.dynamicRoot()` before app code and drop saved app code. Warm Mihon instantiated 4,661 modules instead of 80,305; Calculator's first tap 330 → 65 ms.
- **Resolution goes through the Java loader** (2026-10-07). Recorded initiated identities failed: ChatGPT's GMS loader resolved uninitiated references to the app's stripped Chromium classes, and a child-loader reference returned the parent's class. Resolution calls the actual loader at the use point and caches it; cross-loader signature constraints are checked before class initialization.
- **Defining-loader ownership** (2026-10-07, `0190838c`). Proxies keep the Java-supplied loader; equal names in different loaders stay distinct; inherited platform classes are not the app root's; package access compares defining loaders too.
- **Shared libraries and the loader graph** (2026-10-05). YouTube's `org.apache.http.legacy`: a pending lazy-VFS read was cached as "no DEX" (now awaited), and Java `loadClass` found `BasicHttpParams` while compiler and host lookup did not. `sharedLibraryLoaders`/`…After` define parent/before/own/after order; the same DEX under another defining loader gets its own namespace. A graph change refreshes `MethodInfo`/`FieldInfo` in place, invalidates compiled callers, CHA and app AOT, and rebinds every dependent loader.
- **Native class lookup in ART order** (2026-10-08, `e94dce6a`, `21cdf1e6`). Verified ordinary graphs search parent, shared-before, each DEX, shared-after; custom, cyclic or changed graphs use managed resolution. `VMClassLoader.findLoadedClass` searches recognized path loaders even for unloaded classes, as Android 15 does; checking only initiated ones gave Lichess's `JniPlugin.setClassLoader` `NoClassDefFoundError: null class`.
- **Loader prediction for dynamite modules** (2026-10-12). Firebase loads through a `PathClassLoader` under microG's dex-less `FilteredClassLoader`; a parent-first prediction was contradicted by each define of the module's bundled Kotlin/Play classes (324 corrections, 21 s). A loader predicts its own classes first, after boot, when it is `DelegateLastClassLoader` or an app `ClassLoader` sits between it and its nearest defining ancestor. Resy 61–66 → 32 s.
- **Rebinding scope** (2026-10-07..08). Resy's onboarding-close wedge spent 85% of samples in `rebindResolution`. Rebinding rechecks only loaders depending on the changed namespace plus superclass/interface dependents, including unlinked interfaces in other loaders (same-loader-only fails rollback tests); single-name initiation narrows to matching signatures and inheritance chains. Descriptor-only rebinds keep the subtype index and positive assumptions; absence assumptions always invalidate. Samples 97.2/112.2 → 63.3/83.5 s.
- **Rebinding metadata refresh** (2026-10-08). Missing exact lookups binary-search DEX class/name instead of building every declared key (31.11 → 4.70 ms per 10k). `DexClass.refreshTypes` skips unchanged descriptors (456 → 261 ms; descriptors were already memoized, so allocation was not the cost). Cached headers drop only when their raw super/interface descriptor names the changed binding (491 → 341 ms).
- **Library rebinding touches only moved names** (2026-10-10..11). Shared libraries (`android.test.base` via `PackageBackwardCompatibility`) marked every class of affected loaders changed. `Program.libraryChanges` computes only names whose lookup moves (Mihon warm 9.4 → 8.5 s); the recheck walks declared keys up superclass chains (microG 325 → 175 ms).
- **Main-thread context loader** (2026-10-05, `8124380a`). A cold system-loader initializer reaching `Thread.currentThread` left the peer a null loader; as in ART, the scheduler assigns it after that initializer succeeds, and an explicit set, even null, cancels that.
- **DEX order and packed archives** (2026-10-05..07). Multidex order is numeric, as in ART. `openDexes` reads only DEX from packed archives (`9ed28d3d`); rebuilding all 2,897 GMS files stalled Resy.

### Dispatch, slots and CHA

- **CHA guard for running activations** (2026-10-05). Invalidating a table entry cannot change a direct call in a running module, so a native callback loading a subclass got the old implementation. Direct sites test the receiver's slot first, only where the unit has a direct function; indirect/AOT sites read the slot once.
- **CHA declaration lookup without an indexing cliff** (2026-10-06..08). A full implementor index cost ~190 ms; deferring it to query 128 moved the pause into FitNotes's first previous-day tap (102 → 181 ms). `Index.declaring` caches only requested signatures and finds DEX methods via numeric name IDs: worst lookup 126.4 → 18.5 ms, retained keys 138,421 → 535, tap → ~122 ms. A signature-only variant without the numeric index slowed the bulk probe and was dropped.
- **Declared-member lookup** (2026-10-05). AOT validation (`World.holds` → `Program.implFor`), CHA's wide fallback and method-ID decoding share `declaredMethod`, which scans class data without materializing members (TikTok live heap 1.94 GB → 571 MB; RSS did not fall). `Program.addClass` puts every declared method in `byKey`; a `$$host` class with an empty `byKey` broke only the layered Program.
- **CHA allocation and late classes** (2026-10-11). `World.late` indexed late classes under every supertype (~70 MB per TikTok realm); `lateBy` answers on demand. `Cha.subsOf` concatenated subtype lists per call (1 GB visiting ≤ 64 classes); walks read in place. A class load drops only memos about the new classes' supertypes and below. TikTok CHA 6.1 → 2.0 s, on-demand compiling 29.6 → 19.5 s in 60 s.
- **Host shells and CHA** (2026-10-05, `02e82805`, `a5749f0e`). Interface host shells register with the spec, not at first bridge crossing (earlier code bound the sole bytecode implementor); abstract host classes get a concrete shell before CHA.
- **Flat and shared layouts must agree** (2026-10-07, `94881d0c`, `148c7c34`). Duplicate abstract interface slots made shared-platform layouts differ from flat ones (ThreadPoolExecutor 64/58), a signature mismatch at Resy's `setKeepAliveTime`; abstract declarations are counted once.
- **Package-private dispatch** (2026-10-07, `d4534435`). Package-private declarations get distinct identities per runtime package; qualified slots select via the canonical declaration. The old selector gave `111111` where Java gives `112222` (split loaders); public-child fixtures hid this.
- **Default methods** (2026-10-07, `03e44b63`, `b40fec33`). Defaults select from canonical maximal declarations, not copied members. Concrete-default conflicts prepare and throw a lazy `IncompatibleClassChangeError` at invocation, as ART does (JDK throws AME). Wide CHA continues past an interface match, stops at a class body.
- **Slot identity keys** (2026-10-07..08, `5515b119`). The IMT key `class * 65536 + selector` collided above 65,535 selectors; nested maps keep both. `World.vslots` groups candidates by binary signature before override checks: FitNotes graph 1,047 → 508 ms, entries identical.

### Classes, initialization and monitors

- **Initialization and monitors follow strands** (2026-10-02..03). JThread-only recursion checks exposed partial statics to interleaved jobs. Monitor owners and initializers are `Strand`s (`suspend.ts`); waits form a wait-for graph, partial statics only on a cycle. Host code continuing a chain must carry it (`Cpu.call`; `caller()` for nativeLoad and JNI), else Java waits on its own native caller (Komikku's `ImageDecoder.<clinit>`).
- **Forked runtimes must not shadow the strand** (2026-10-03). `Runtime.within` stored the strand on a DexClassLoader fork's `cur`, so a monitor entered as one thread exited as another and threw IllegalMonitorStateException forever (YouTube never drew); it writes the root's slot.
- **System before SystemClock** (2026-10-05). `SystemClock.<clinit>` reads `System.nanoTime`, and a job racing `System.<clinit>` read null properties; `startMainLooper` initializes System first, as the zygote does.
- **Lookup and identity rules** (2026-09-26..10-03). Inherited static lookup must not rerun a superclass initializer; constructors do not inherit. A static belongs to its declaring class (a pinned native static nulled `Rect.CREATOR` via `Region`). A failed `<clinit>` gives EIIE once, NCDFE after. Arrays hash and compare by identity (`0ab7fe1a`).

### Reflection and stack introspection

- **Reflection semantics** (2026-09-28..10-07). Reflective calls widen Integer to long; `getDeclaredMethod` excludes inherited methods (LinkedHashMap serialized HashMap's data twice); fields keep declared types and final statics; `getConstructors` is public-only. MethodHandle lookup must not reuse public reflection, which adds inherited interface methods and drops package-private ones (`8b6991e8`).
- **Annotation materialization can suspend** (2026-10-05). Annotation constructors, boxing and enum initialization hit SuspendError under a plain callback; they run through `World.callFn` on the captured strand after saving caller state (`src/wasm/link/annotations.ts`).
- **Executable and Class metadata** (2026-10-05). Wrapping `Executable.artMethod`'s 64-bit cookie to 32 bits threw for class IDs above 65,535. `ClassValue` failed in ChatGPT because a native wrote host-only `JClass.ext` onto a Wasm Class; the intrinsic publishes the real `extData`, as ART does.
- **Stack-class lookups** (2026-10-02..13). `Reflection.getCallerClass` reads V8 wasm frame names (none in JavaScriptCore); `aotstore` fixes the URL names `compileStreaming` gives. `VMStack.getClosestUserClassLoader` walks to the first non-boot loader. Trace lines resolve from the frame's pc when read.

### MethodHandle, VarHandle and Unsafe

- **MethodHandle, VarHandle, indy** (2026-10-07, `bbfc79fa`). Transformers run unchanged libcore bytecode; Android 15 has no `invokeBasic`/`linkTo`. Bootstraps run the real Java methods, allow races, cache no failures; CallSite targets are read per call. Atomic RMW needs raw accessors installed eagerly (a host export in the Wasm table was rejected); exact VarHandle sites bypass the bridge; MethodHandle dispatch uses the nested-entry quota.
- **VarHandle checks** (`5692dfb3`, `699ca7b4`, `ccd5dcbe`). The access mode comes from the boot enum field, not `AccessMode.valueFromMethodName`; type checks compare live Class mirrors (960,000 fewer `classOf` calls). Mismatched reference descriptors are admitted when operands are null: a null CAS had gone through `AsTypeAdapter` capturing stacks (15.07 M before Guava's first test), and Guava Maps then finished in 293.4 s instead of timing out.
- **Unsafe field cookies are 8-aligned** (2026-10-05). YouTube masks a boolean offset with `-4` and extracts the byte from `getInt`; cookies advancing by one aliased a long field. They advance by eight. Mixed native offset domains remain unverified.
- **Unsafe accessor cache in Wasm** (2026-10-09). Each Unsafe get/put/CAS rebuilt a key and closure (405,263 per 15 s Mihon scroll). Validated slots live in a Wasm i32 array keyed by cookie, operation and kind. 500,000 lookups 66.7–85.7 → 1.1–1.9 ms; scroll gains not established.

### Host classes and the spec boundary

- **Host code must keep overridable calls** (2026-09-28..10-03). `bun run bypass` found 786 TS methods skipping an override AOSP calls and an app declares (`onScrollChanged` never ran), and 406 platform `toString`/`equals`/`hashCode` answered with Object's. Host overrides set only under the public mangled name were skipped by compiled code; `implement()` installs both.
- **TS spec boundary** (2026-10-05). Platform specs fell 291 → 80; `desccheck` fails a spec method with an AOSP body outside `TS_SPECS` (`provenance.ts`). Flipping a spec exposes service calls the TS class never made. A class in `rt.markers` shadows the AOSP class.
- **Host construction of bytecode classes** (2026-10-12..13). `rt.make` of a bytecode class is a field carrier that cannot take virtual calls. A whole-host class's empty `<clinit>` overlay overwrote spec values (`EGL10.EGL_NO_CONTEXT` null); it applies only to an untaken initializer.

### Heap, memory and retention

- **Heap stays Wasm GC** (2026-10-05). Kernels favoured engine GC over a linear-memory collector with retained objects, and no measured loss was attributed to the GC heap.
- **Object model limits** (2026-10-04). Weak and soft references keep referents until cleared; none is enqueued. V8 accepts Wasm GC objects in `WeakMap`/`FinalizationRegistry`. Wasm cannot catch V8's stack overflow, so a frame-weight limit raises StackOverflowError.
- **Committed Wasm memories stall V8's GC** (2026-10-02). Committed memory counts as V8 external memory; near ~2x old-gen max marking restarts as it finishes (two 4 GiB memories: allocation 18x slower). A `byte[]` made before native code initialises is a JS-heap array (`HostBytes`), and guest memory is sized only when native code starts (Mihon tap GC 21.0 → 0.38–0.56 s).
- **Direct buffers, weak globals, owned handles** (2026-09-30..10-04). Direct buffers live in core memory (`VMRuntime.addressOf` had made every `byte[]` a permanent table entry). JNI weak globals are `WeakRef`s. `FinalizationRegistry` never returns the object, so creating natives return owned handles freed when their first owner is collected.
- **Closures retain their Compiler** (2026-10-05..08). `World.callFn` helpers shared a scope with the emitter callback, so 1,000 helpers kept 1,000 Compilers; a separate activation cut a sample heap 58.2 → 31.8 MB. `symbolicSites` resolvers, parsed AOT headers and traces of non-Java helpers are released (YouTube header samples 55.8 → 7.2 MB).

## Code delivery and tiering

### App builds and persistence

- **Saved launch builds** (2026-10-05). A warm FitNotes launch compiled ~2.9k methods on demand and held the main thread 450 ms on a 130 MB streaming compile. A saved app build holds what a launch built on demand, and platform builds ship profiled apps' builds: warm 3.53 → 1.74–1.87 s, cold 4.06 → 2.15–2.55 s (loaded host, SwiftShader).
- **First launch without a saved build** (2026-10-11/12). Feeder took 3.2 s vs 0.76 s later; of 2.0 s compiling, 1.4 s was the compiler front end and 0.5 s instantiating a module per method. Even warm, Mihon recompiles 28 platform methods because an app class overrides one that platform code calls directly (`javax/net`).
- **Rejected: waiting for a parallel first app build** (2026-10-10). Six Workers building for a cold launch cut Calculator's first taps 181–187 → 38–48 ms, but slowed FitNotes 2.48 → 2.61–2.81 s and Mihon 9.6 → 10.5 s: the build (1.7× one Worker's CPU) displaced the zygote's overlap with prefetch. A one-Worker build is worse (Feeder ~5 vs 3.2 s); an install-time build pays only split across Workers, each holding the app's Program, which TikTok's size rules out.
- **App builds with shared libraries** (2026-10-10/11). `appLaunched` fires on a first frame, so service processes never persisted builds; a 15 s start window now starts the watch. A build started with empty `AppIndex.libraries` was unbound by the first `bindLibraries`; it now starts from `watch` with the bound graph. Warm Mihon's microG inline compiles 2,491/351 → 13/0.
- **Kept builds and the library binding** (2026-10-13). Every warm YouTube launch died in `handleBindApplication` with `LinkageError: Class loader libraries changed linked hierarchy of areh`. The attached build's data module had linked `areh`, laid out under the shared libraries the build was made under, before `PathClassLoader` bound them; `rebindResolution` checked hierarchy and dispatch before consulting `keepApp`. With the build kept, classes laid out by its data modules (`ClassRt.mod` `e…`) skip those checks; classes linked on demand still fail. aottest covers an app copy of a class the library defines first. Warm YouTube then launched in 5.3 s (cold 14 s) with first responses 7–168 ms (cold 205–445 ms).
- **App builds must not duplicate reserved classes** (2026-10-08). Intermittent warm FitNotes StackOverflowError: an app data module replaced live cached code (`tagsoup/Parser.setProperty`) with a lazy-link stub while the slot said compiled, so the stub called itself. The builder uses the canonical `reservedBase` query; cache v12 rejects older builds. Not proven to explain every older overflow.
- **Refine share** (2026-10-12). A refine rebuilds the whole merged demand in a fresh Worker; TikTok's service process rebuilt ~80,000 methods for a few hundred new ones at peak main heap, crashing the shared cage. A refine waits until new demand is an eighth of the build (`REFINE_SHARE`).
- **Refinement during input: not integrated** (2026-10-09). Each Mihon scroll window completed a refinement (~30,800 methods, 6.4–6.6 s of builder work); input gating contradicted itself (52.73 vs 55.26, then 55.93 vs 54.87 presentations/s); an incremental `fresh` poll cut poll work 9.6 → 0.1 ms but not pacing (55.48 vs 55.11; reverted). The pacing cause is open.

### Platform build

- **Launch profiles and fewer launch modules** (2026-10-10). FitNotes' first launch entered platform methods 535k times, its own 49k (2026-10-01). Workers compile the platform C/D modules an app's launches loaded before its next launch (FitNotes 2430 → ~1900 ms). `hot.json` groups called classes into fewer C modules; runtime functions are precompiled into `y<k>.wasm`/`s<k>.wasm`, not one module each (−150 ms).
- **Source key and integrity** (2026-10-05). `host/natives.ts` emits import signatures but was outside the key, which now covers all production TypeScript and AOT scripts by path/mtime/size (max mtime missed deletions). A retry path that dropped functions V8 rejected now aborts the build.
- **Fallback class globals** (2026-10-08). Warm FitNotes had 258 synchronous link misses in one tap, all in platform code groups rejected because their data group had published fallback globals. The linker binds those imports through the canonical class-import binder and tries cached app patches after rejection; late data never replaces live class state. Launch compilations ~5,980 → 309.
- **Late data and prefetch** (2026-10-06..13). Late platform C modules link only against their original data globals. `AOT.ready` marks an unavailable module permanently missed, so a no-app realm awaits prefetch. Warm launches hung when > 16 modules were prefetched: `FetchStore.load` returned early on each completion's empty list (from `e951d359`).
- **Rejected: every boot jar in the platform build** (2026-10-14, plan 1.8). By jar, a cold launch compiled 302 methods (FitNotes) and 356 (Komikku) from boot jars the build leaves out: `framework-connectivity` 61–135, `ext` 83 (FitNotes), `framework-statsd` 45, `framework-connectivity-t` 22–32, and fewer from 16 others. Cold taps compiled 0–3 such methods and warm launches 4, since an app build keeps them. A build of all 40 boot jars (`aot --jars 40`: 54,727 classes, 265 MB against 205 MB) compiled about 300 fewer methods in a cold launch and launched FitNotes faster in each of three alternating `--gpu` pairs (medians 1,887 vs 2,073 ms) and Komikku by about 2.5% over six (6,160 vs 6,323 ms). Cold Komikku `appearance` was slower in five of six pairs, though, with the same methods compiled and modules loaded in the tap (median 160 vs 136 ms, past the gate), and the cause was not found, so the build keeps its eight jars.
- **An unprofiled build misses the zygote's modules** (2026-10-14). `bun run aot` alone writes `aot-startup`'s list, whose harness `startProcess` makes three Java calls: 199 files, none of the C modules a browser `zygoteInit` links. Warm Komikku waited for 70 code deliveries (~250 ms) per launch, each realm again. A process Worker fetching and compiling its own modules waited as long (218–226 vs 250–266 ms), so the page's relay was not the delay. After `aot-profile --classes`, `bun run aot` and `aot-profile` (397 files, `warm.json`), misses fell 46 → 12 and warm launch 3.0–3.4 s → 2.45–2.6 s. Measure warm launches only on a profiled build.

### Launch path, zygote and spare

- **Zygote before the package** (2026-10-11). An app build records its `prefix` (what the World numbered past the platform build before app dexes) and attaches only to a World that numbered exactly that, or nothing. Zygote numbering is deterministic, so the spare Worker, spawned at page load, runs the zygote before the package is known: Calculator 1,373–1,399 → 768–779 ms from `wd.boot()`. With `?apk=` it waits: an early system_server boot competed with the app's AOT loads.
- **Zygote numbering depends on timing** (2026-10-13). Every warm TikTok launch rejected its saved build: `prefix: names […]`, the same five classes (`TrafficStats$1`, `TrafficStats$SocketTagger`, three `$$host` overlays) in a different order. All five are numbered on the main thread by `preForkInit`/`commonInit`, so this is not a thread race. When `RuntimeInit.commonInit`'s C module has arrived, `loadC`'s class imports build the pending classes and their vtables link the overlays first. When it has not, `commonInit` compiles on demand and links `SocketTagger` first. A process now keeps what it numbered before its app's code (`Persist.prefix`, per platform build) and the next process numbers that first through `World.adopt`. Timing then only appends, and the kept list grows until it stops changing. Warm TikTok attaches its build: 287 methods compiled at launch instead of 27,154, and launch 12.3 s → 8.2 s. The claim in the entry above, that zygote numbering is deterministic, does not hold.
- **Spare refill policy** (2026-10-11). Refilling after every process start chained spares that each warmed and ran a zygote: FitNotes 9–21 scroll intervals > 25 ms vs 0–1, 0.9 s CPU per unused spare. A new spare starts only when a launch takes one whose warm had not completed.
- **Bitness before zygoteInit** (2026-10-02). `System.<clinit>` and `Build.<clinit>` read the ABI, so `startZygote` loads the platform app-free and awaits bitness from the install's central directories (known at 368–478 ms, before `zygoteInit`). Fixing it in `bootApk` would serialize `zygoteInit` (~0.4 s warm, 2.5 s throttled) behind the download.
- **Platform-first class path** (2026-09-30). PathClassLoader asks the boot loader first, so the platform is indexed and linked at page start, overlapping the APK download: cold first frame −150 to −220 ms.
- **Rejected: boot image** (2026-10-05). Of the zygote's 453 ms to `startZygote`, 12 ms was Java and 172 ms module loading. A heap loader would save ≤ 8%: statics live in globals of modules it instantiates anyway.

### Modules and instantiation

- **What a module costs** (2026-10-12/13). V8 charges 37–40 KB of external memory per instantiated module with code (a code-space page for jump tables), 7.5 KB globals-only, freed once its functions are unreferenced; external memory drives full collections. No V8 lazy/tiering/Liftoff flag changes Chrome's per-module compile cost, so the lever is fewer modules.
- **Fewer modules per on-demand compile** (2026-10-12). Classes linked on demand are globals-only modules with slots stubbed from pools shared by signature; units export base-typed helpers later units import; class globals linked since the last read form one module. Komikku launch modules ~23,000 → ~17,700.
- **Entry batches kept, callee folding rejected** (2026-10-12/13). `World.batch` adds a method's static/direct calls before its first branch (≤ 32), yet Calculator's 3,957 links compiled 3,711 methods; over TikTok's demand static batching removes ≤ 45% of modules and compiles unrun methods. 64% of unlinked single-target callees were linked later; folding saves ~85 µs per hit and wastes ~200 µs per miss, breaking even near 70%.
- **Merging first-tier units** (2026-10-12). Once 1,024 units are loose the upgrade Worker recompiles up to 512 of their methods into one module; calls stay through the table, so invalidation is per method. Merges wait for the first frame and any app build, then idle twice their duration (back to back, a Worker reached 1 GB in 20 s). TikTok's first 100 s: 97 mark-compacts (12.7 s) → 26–35 (1.5–1.8 s). Merging in launch cost Feeder 70 ms.
- **Engine recompiles after a merge** (2026-10-14). A cold Komikku tap ran 725–3,170 `wasm.CompileLazy` events (V8 compiling a function on its first call), 9–57 ms of 46–130 ms taps; a CPU profile charges them to the calling Java frame. Most were in functions at indices 100–999, which only merged modules reach: a merge re-emits already-run methods into a new module whose functions the engine compiles again when next called. With merging off they fell to 7.5–15.6 ms per tap, but taps did not get faster (sums of tap medians 534 vs 536 ms over three alternating pairs): the compile worker then answered more second-tier jobs inside the taps.
- **Rejected: merging only dormant units** (2026-10-14). Taking into a merge only units whose call counts had not changed for 5 s, so running methods keep the code the engine compiled, left cold Komikku taps unchanged (sums of tap medians 524 vs 519 ms over three alternating pairs).
- **Freeing merged-away units stalls the thread** (2026-10-13). Cold Komikku taps intermittently stalled 80–170 ms with the Worker silent: no timeline event, no rAF, not even the 33 ms fallback timer. A GC-category trace (`WEBDROID_TRACE_CATEGORIES=disabled-by-default-v8.gc,v8`) showed one task, `V8.GC_HEAP_EXTERNAL_SECOND_PASS_CALLBACKS`, covering each stall. A FinalizationRegistry count found ~3,950 first-tier unit instances freed in a single tap window. A merge leaves the units it replaces unreferenced; between major collections thousands accumulate, and the engine frees each module's native code in the second-pass weak callbacks after the collection, all in one task. Every traced cold run had one or two such tasks (90–170 ms); keeping every instance alive removed them. `World.retire` now holds the units a merge replaces and drops them `RETIRE_BATCH` (32) at a time, the next batch once a FinalizationRegistry reports the last collected, or after 5 s. The largest second-pass task fell to 3–8 ms (10–17 ms with batches of 128). The longest silent stretch in any tap fell from 79–169 ms to ≤ 24 ms over 10 cold runs. The units are released over minutes instead of at once, so the external memory merging frees arrives later. `wasmRetired`/`wasmCollected` count the drain.
- **Flat statics are not a tap lever** (2026-10-14, plan 5.1). Cold Komikku taps built 13–87 classes in 0.3–2.4 ms, and all their modules took 3–17 ms to create; the launch built ~7,340 classes in 195–218 ms of 16k modules' 810 ms. Flat statics would remove class modules and most imports, which bounds the gain to part of that launch time and a few milliseconds per tap.
- **Reverted: synthetic stubs per header** (2026-10-11). ~550 of a realm's ~1,250 pre-first-frame modules are single-function lazy-link stubs (`ensureSynth`); one module per header cut warm Calculator ~1,605 → ~1,565 ms. Reverted over e2e settle failures later traced to a harness clock fault; marking stubbed slots done on loading an app build's synthetic module (`holdsSynth`) crashed the renderer.
- **Rejected: inherited vtable targets** (2026-10-14). `vtWords` took 218 ms of a profiled cold Komikku launch, mostly resolving each slot's target. Reusing a superclass's targets for slots a class and its added interfaces do not declare must read `byKey`, not `methods` (a `$$host` overlay keeps methods it hosts for a superclass only there; `hostclass` caught `HttpsHandler.openConnection`), and must drop kept targets whenever classes load. Correct that way it saved nothing (cold launch 6239 vs 6240 ms, three alternating pairs); app classes mostly extend platform classes, whose vtables come from the platform build.
- **Rejected: one array supertype for `array-length`** (2026-10-14). Plan 3.4's single cast: a `$Array` struct whose immutable data field is `(ref array)`, as every element kind's wrapper's supertype, so `array-length` is one `ref.cast` and `array.len` instead of up to four `ref.test`s. Steady state was unchanged in node g1 (ten workloads, both directions within ±5% under load ~4), and modules grew 8 B on average because every module declares the base types. The type tests are cheap once optimized.
- **Rejected: one prologue call** (2026-10-14). Plan 3.1 without its `WARM_AT` change: a method that both counts its frame and its calls made one `enterCount(slot, weight)` call instead of `enter` then `count`. Node g1 over 74 workloads was neutral (first calls 1.007×, steady 1.001× geomean), and the benchmark's modules shrank 1 B per method. TurboFan inlines both helpers, and most first-tier methods are leaves that only count.

### Engine compilation and code cache

- **Engine code cache does not help** (2026-10-11). A 12.5 MB module compiled eagerly in 75–86 ms per launch from an immutable HTTP response and Cache Storage alike. Chrome refuses synchronous `new WebAssembly.Instance` over 8 MB on a page's main thread and a 204 MB set failed to fetch, so chunks stay under 8 MiB. Images compile once per tab and are posted to Workers.
- **Warming engine code in the spare** (2026-10-11). Lazy Liftoff was the largest launch cost (~20k compiles, 259 ms); eager compilation cannot ship and Chrome ignores hints. `World.warm` calls the 18,117 methods of `warm.json` with zero arguments while a linear-memory word makes `count` trap on entry, warming the tab-shared `Module`: Calculator 786–797 → 615–620 ms. Completed before boot it made FitNotes 1,746 vs 1,578 ms, so it runs in 10 ms slices and stops at `bootApk`. A helper Worker cannot warm app code: only a World numbering the same classes supplies an instance's GC-typed globals.

### Compiling ahead

- **Compiling ahead in idle time** (2026-10-13). `World.ahead` compiles on the app thread after the looper has waited 100 ms with no other thread runnable and no input for 1 s, in ≤ 4 ms slices: single-target callees of methods that ran, transitively (16–45% of a cold tap's links; one level only 10–25%). Gating on the looper alone cost Komikku's launch ~70 ms, and a 250 ms input window its `start` tap 273 → 320 ms; with a saved build it slowed Calculator's first warm tap 27 → 41 ms, so it runs only without one. Cold medians: Calculator `7` 179 → 127, Komikku `appearance` 337 → 264, `start` 267 → 291 ms (ranges overlap).
- **What a cold heavy-app tap compiles** (2026-10-13). Cold YouTube taps compile 1,000–1,850 first-tier methods (`you` 205–297 ms, `settings` 387–445 ms to the first response), about one module per method: `World.batch` takes only straight-line direct calls. In the profiled `you` window (296 ms), compiling and installing took 127 ms (`buildUnit` 74, synchronous `WebAssembly.Module` 41), guest ARM 58, main-thread upgrade planning 16 (`likely`/`allocatedSubtypes`) and first-tier Java 110. `lat --demand` records each tap's on-demand methods (`World.demandSince`). The app's baseline profile (37.8k methods) names 6–33% of them. 50–95% are in classes linked before the tap, but those classes then held about 91k methods without code. The call frontier of methods with code (22.6k, every target) covered 12–30%, single targets 10–20%, and the frontier with the profile 15–50%. `ahead` had reached `AHEAD_MAX` (8,192) during launch. Prediction cannot take such taps off the compiler; the cost per method and per module can.
- **Compiling ahead runs once per wait** (2026-10-14). The input gate is checked once, 100 ms into a looper wait, when the input that started the work is always under 1 s old, so after the first tap `ahead` effectively stops (Komikku: 0–87 methods per tap window). Waiting out the window while the looper still waits ran it after every tap, and then the call frontier emptied within 85–165 methods; adding the remaining methods of linked classes that ran filled `AHEAD_MAX` in two processes yet left each tap's on-demand count unchanged (`more` 709). Waiting out the window alone made cold Komikku taps slower (medians of 3, alternating: `start` 105 → 139, `more` 132 → 145, `appearance` 148 → 161 ms; Calculator mixed), so the single check stays.
- **Rejected: compiling ahead in a Worker** (2026-10-12). Compiling callees of on-demand methods in the idle compile Worker made Komikku's launch 8,645–8,670 → 8,792–8,911 ms, on-demand compiling unchanged: the app thread reaches most methods first, adopting a unit costs ~1.1 ms and renumbering more. Only relocatable code would free ahead units from the page World's numbering.
- **Rejected: seeded app numbering to attach the first build mid-session** (2026-10-14). Plan 2.1, for a cold launch, whose first app build arrives ~30 s after Komikku's first frame. `World.seedApp` numbered, after the prefix and in dex order, app classes (an id and a `methodCount+1` slot block), the dexes' array types and interface selectors, in the page and the build Worker. With the Worker's numbering frozen, 99.9% of Komikku's 131,921 methods compiled without a new number (FitNotes 22,376 of 22,395); the rest named link errors (94), `$$host` overlays (33) or a platform class outside the build (1). Seeding took 23 ms for Komikku (slots 365k → 532k) and 4 ms for FitNotes; TikTok's 462k classes took 474 ms and need 2.83M slots, past `COUNT_SLOTS`. Warm launches were neutral. A build of code modules alone attached 4.0 s after the first frame with method-granular selection (30–75% of each cold tap's on-demand methods) or 6.4–7.3 s class-granular (63–88%). Data modules cannot attach late: their stubs and `ensure` functions overwrite slots the page already compiled, and their vtables name synthetic slots beyond the seed. Taps compiled less (`more` 41 → 20–39 ms), but a late code module links every class it names when loaded (6,657 classes; 180–245 ms in the next tap with 1 MB modules, 59–68 ms with 64 KB), so the first tap after attaching took 140–263 ms against 45–91 ms and the rest stayed within noise. A cold tap is mostly not compiling: unprofiled `appearance` took 155–219 ms with 15–22 ms compiled; profiled, `linkSlot` (emit, `WebAssembly.Module`, instantiate) was 59 ms of `more`'s 261 ms and of `appearance`'s 285 ms, the rest Java (Compose recomposition, coil loads on coroutine threads) and, in `appearance`, 97 ms idle.
- **Earlier compile-ahead trials** (2026-09-29..10-03, JS tier). Compiling Mihon's 18,274-method baseline profile at boot cut the first tap 147 → 124 ms but moved the first frame 2.2 → 3.3 s. RTA from the launcher Activity needs its first 8,000 of 20,117 app methods (37% precision) to cover 78% of a Calculator M launch. 20,000 methods ahead cut Komikku's first-tap cold calls 12,256 → 5,050 (198 → 185 ms); newest-first beat oldest-first; starting beside the zygote or after the first frame was worse.

### Second tier and hot upgrades

- **Repeat taps bound a shipped second tier** (2026-10-14, plans 0.5 and 2.3). Komikku's Appearance screen opened ten times in one session (back, then Appearance) took 167, 93 and 52 ms on its first, second and tenth visits cold, and 82, 64 and 50 ms warm; cold compiling fell from 33 ms to 0–6 ms after the first visit. Second-tier code shipped in app builds could take a warm first visit from about 82 toward 50–60 ms, a tap already well inside the gate; a cold launch has no app build to ship it in. Not pursued.
- **Second tier kept** (2026-10-10/13). Without it, steady state fell from 1.59× to 0.82× of the removed JS tier on Bench. Hot methods overflowing the Worker's 16-job queue ask later rather than compile inline; planning waits for the starting window (`holdUpgrades`); virtual calls with > 32 implementations get no guards (`39bbe2f1`): Mihon `upgradeJob` 217 → 61 ms.
- **Inline upgrade policy** (2026-10-06). Timing-dependent upgrades made workloads bimodal; leaves wait, cost is a fixed 1 ms, and the oldest still-hot method and outermost frame go first. Guards cover implementations that ran (Chrome listIter 0.145 → 0.080 ms).
- **Hot-unit refresh** (2026-10-07, `c6d40dff`). Hot units compiled with unresolved symbolic imports are refreshed once dependencies are ready: late steady MapInt 0.502 → 0.153 ms, first calls unchanged. Rejected: publishing requested hot copies slowed both workloads without AOT.
- **Rejected promotion policies** (2026-10-08). A 12-method group cap nearly doubled deque steady; large roots alone worsened JSON; active-callers-only starved promotion; starting the budget at first Java entry raised boot+first on all ten primary workloads; cost per root word starved HashMap; whole-method promotion with saturated loop counters re-ran `ZipFile$Source.initCEN` (listIter 0.2191 → 0.5036 ms). Open: the oldest-refused override can pick `initCEN`, and the 1 ms admission estimate admits 21–25 ms compiles.
- **Planning indexes** (2026-10-08/09). Planning filtered the allocated-class set per queried base (7,166,810 classes per Mihon scroll window); each newly allocated class is now indexed once under its supertypes: 70.0 → 2.9 ms per window, intervals > 25 ms 48/39 → 19/20. `World.likely` keeps the two busiest targets instead of sorting (38.9 → 28.0 ms).

### Compile Workers

- **Bounded compiler Workers** (2026-10-05). ChatGPT's compiler Workers each built Program metadata and a World (729–791 MB each); four crashed the renderer. App builds take an origin-wide Web Lock and transfer modules before termination; the upgrade service starts on the first hot request. The app Worker alone held 1.41 GB, which this does not bound.
- **Worker follows Proxies and DEX namespaces** (2026-10-08/09). Annotation Proxies and non-root `World.addDexes` retired the Worker, moving Mihon's hot compilation to the main thread. Proxy definitions now cross as wire data and the protocol mirrors DEX ownership and library namespaces (a bootstrap-only version failed: Mihon's Proxy belongs to a `BootClassLoader` object). Main-thread scroll compile 732.8/845.2 → 476.8/373.5 ms; FPS unchanged. Other host additions still retire it (a stale Worker is unsound).
- **Worker answers wait out input** (2026-10-14, plan 1.7). ~6 compile-worker answers (second-tier or merged units, each a main-thread adoption plus the planning of the next job) landed inside each cold Komikku response window. `CompileClient` now holds planning and adoption while the realm answers input (`answeringFor`, 250 ms after the latest pointer event). Two series of three alternating pairs: sums of tap medians 541 vs 621 and 561 vs 626 ms (`start` 92–123 vs 163–167, `more` 133–135 vs 152–155); `settings`, which follows a swipe, was slower (107–132 vs 92–97) as fewer upgrades were in place by then. Holding only adoption and planning during input measured the same (548 vs 540). A third series at `a5e24b4bb` (four alternating pairs, the hold switched by a temporary page parameter on one server) agreed: every run with the hold was faster than its pair (sums 486–569 vs 551–582 ms), medians summed 503 vs 578 ms, and without the hold `appearance`'s median was 155 ms (130 with it).

## Host boundary, threads and scheduling

### Strands and thread identity across waits

- **Resume as the thread that waited** (2026-09-28..10-05). Work resuming after an await ran as whatever thread was current: two Java threads shared one native CPU, service continuations ran off main (microG, Komikku), Reddit's launch waited on a class its own thread was initializing, and Amazon's cookie handler after `await fetch` entered a monitor main had released by parking, so javac's catch-all re-ran its own `monitor-exit` forever. Driven generators re-enter the calling thread at each resume; `.then` and host-timer callbacks are the same hazard; a thread is released when its work suspends.
- **Restore strands inside resumed Wasm** (2026-10-07). Restoring sid/thread/depth in a Promise reaction is too early, since another completion can idle the globals first. `5ad21add` restores inside the resumed stub, exceptional exits included; `2b23baf2`/`e9e8d126` do the same after an immediate suspending-import exception.
- **Exact strand on nested and resumed entry** (2026-10-07..12). A fast nested JNI callback cleared the guest CPU turn's strand, losing monitor reentry (Resy deadlocked in Mapbox `nativeLoad`): `onStrand` restores the exact strand in a synchronous finally. Async host-entry continuations capture caller Thread and native parent (`a83afdb5`; a thread-only fix lost monitor ancestry). `MediaCodecList.initCodecList` holds `sInitLock` across a wait, and resuming as the thread's root strand deadlocked it on itself: `asThread` resumes as the parent's host strand.
- **Detached entries and Looper identity** (2026-10-03). Fire-and-forget Java entries use `detached(call, escape)`; `scripts/generators.ts` rejects a `void` or bare-statement entry. A process's calling identity is one token (the main Looper once kept the zygote's uid and logged "Thread identity changed" per message).

### Natives that wait (JSPI)

- **Suspending versus plain imports** (2026-10-04..10). No JS frame may sit between a `promising` entry and a suspending import. A native that may wait (a `driven` generator, an async function, or listed in `MAY_SUSPEND`) gets a Suspending import (`src/wasm/host/suspend.ts`); others stay plain, as a Suspending import costs ~50 ns against 4 ns. Conditional waiters (`NativeConverter.charsetForName`, `FontFileUtil` reads) must be declared. Returning Promises only when waiting, and no main-thread fuel-check yield before its turn is due, cut Calculator's launch from 49.7k to 1.3k Suspending calls; launch time barely changed.
- **Import ABI is fixed by name** (2026-10-05). An emitted plain import was rebound as suspending when its implementation was async and resumed under the root strand. `#s` suspends, `#p` is the conditional landing form, unsuffixed imports park explicitly. The plain call saves strand context before entering the native. Always wrap with `Sched.suspending`, never raw `WebAssembly.Suspending` (bypassed nested-stack reset: 1.56M emergency-yield captures); its quota resets only when the import returns a Promise.
- **Never cache a pending suspension** (2026-10-02..05). A pending promise carries its asker's frames: a `??=` memo of `theNetwork` wedged Harmonic, Reddit, TikTok, ChatGPT and YouTube; `applicationInfoOf` caching `rt.drive`'s result hung Mihon's first launch 7/22; a cached service constructor made a second `pms()` wait on itself. `once` shares a computation, `shareable` gives each caller its own continuation stack, and `scripts/generators.ts` flags pending results kept by `??=`, `||=`, `Map.set` or stores.
- **Promises must not reach Java values** (2026-10-05..13). `DisplayEventReceiver.nativeInit` stored a pending `WeakReference.get`, failing at vsync; GMS got host Promises from `getPublicFieldRecursive` callbacks and `newArrayList` (`InternalError`). These now drive the generator/`after`. `JavaClass.new` zero-allocates without class init (a pending allocation killed Resy's GMS). Host code with no Java frame below enters on a new strand that suspends when a lazy link yields after 8 ms; `box`'s Promise reached a cursor row and aborted the owner; `valueOf` now links synchronously.
- **Heap pressure in synchronous entries** (2026-10-08). Over budget in a host byte-array conversion, the allocation slow path tried to suspend across a JS frame and raised `SuspendError` instead of OOM (YouTube Shorts), even for an immediate return. Synchronous entries collect, recheck and throw OOM; suspendable callers keep two timer turns.
- **The facade's methods cannot wait** (2026-10-15). Calling a Java method on its facade runs Java synchronously. In YouTube's Litho layout thread, minikin's `BreakIterator.next()` reached Thai text, whose break engine paged ICU's dictionary in, and raised `SuspendError`. A host loop over Java that can reach a lazy page, class or lock calls through `vcall`. In Chrome that costs 2.2 µs a boundary against the facade's 0.65 µs (a strand entry builds a JSPI stack); boundaries stay cached per text. Under node's test runner the per-entry cost grew round after round from its promise hooks, which a browser does not have.
- **A waiting native holds only its own scope** (2026-10-15). `Bridge.hold` used to set aside every open scope, including those of the natives that had entered Java on the waiting strand's way (a binder transaction into an in-process service, a `vcall` from a driven native). Those natives then went on and ended on an empty scope stack, so their copied arrays were never stored back, and the copies stayed in `live` for other natives to write into. A YouTube walk ended a scope on an empty stack about 6,000 times. The parked path also ended its scope once on return and again on resumption. `hold` now takes the innermost scope's undo entries, live copies and scratch bounds, and its resume function opens them over whatever is open then; `end` closes the innermost scope from a marks stack, and a native that held does not end its scope on return.
- **Rejected: synchronous outer native driver** (2026-10-09). Completing `hostForm`'s immediate values synchronously gained nothing (56.66/54.86 vs 56.40/55.66 presentations/s; FitNotes exercise list 188.5/194.8 vs 195.8/196.4 ms): 6,582 of 6,848 native scopes returned Promises anyway. Find remaining wait producers and nested callback paths first. Most Compose-app suspensions are legitimate (coroutine parks, contended monitors, background SQLite).
- **A reloaded page stayed in memory** (2026-10-15, `c5e53b56`). After one Google Maps launch, navigating the tab to `about:blank` left the renderer's main isolate holding 135 MB of heap and 455 MB of ArrayBuffers, retained from `(Stack roots)` through `WasmImportData` into the bundle's closures (`wd.vfs` files, CanvasKit's heap, jar promises, string pools). A standalone page confirmed the engine behavior: a stack suspended on a Promise that never settles stays a root after navigation, with everything its frames reach, and settling it in `pagehide` frees it. Same-site reloads reuse the renderer, so pages added up: three Maps reloads held 253, 373 and 491 MB of heap (897, 1341 and 1787 MB backing), and a Chromium reloaded for about an hour held 8.1 GB, with launches spending ~78% of the page's time in GC (first frame 22.8 s against 9.8 s fresh). `module.ts` now keeps each pending wait's reject; on `pagehide` they all reject, as does any later wait. Natives pass that error on unconverted and `yieldNow` rethrows it, so the stacks unwind without Java handlers. The first version hung Maps' unload: `startMainLooper` restarts `Looper.loop` after an uncaught exception, and each restart threw at once, a microtask loop. Host entries (`callOn`) now never return once the waits have ended, as after a process exit. Ending the page realm through `exitProcess` was rejected because its listeners would also record and save a `system_server` exit. Worker realms keep the process-exit rule (suspended frames never resume); the page terminates them. After the fix, reloads stay at 135 MB of heap and 454 MB backing, and `about:blank` keeps 1 MB after Maps or FitNotes. e2e and `lat` open fresh pages, so their numbers were unaffected.

### JNI and guest natives

- **Guest calls carry the caller's chain** (2026-10-02, `6a755f69`). `callGuestNative` and `loadModule` awaited before capturing the chain, so the guest ran as a fresh chain waiting on a lock its native caller held: `KevaImpl.<clinit>`'s `JNI_OnLoad` and YouTube's video-open wedged. A later `removeSyncBarrier` IllegalStateException (2/17 loaded runs) did not recur in 15 runs: not reproduced, not fixed.
- **Synchronous JNI entry** (2026-10-02..03, `1a500de2`, `0abb33aa`). `Call<Type>Method` parks only for class init or a suspended callback (100k calls 64.71 → 47.60 ms); a JNI call enters the guest without awaiting when the base frame is free and completes on its caller's stack if done in the first slice (0.70 → 0.37 µs per trivial call). App effect was noise (Doodle Jump 33.2 → 34.4 fps). Parking, signals or a spent slice still suspend the caller.
- **JNI semantics** (2026-10-05..08). `FindClass` enters the referring class's loader on a nested strand without initializing (ART) and leaves a pending exception on failure; an abstract target raises AbstractMethodError (`87facb0e`, per JDK 17). Suspended native array results travel through per-call tokens and resumed Wasm restores scratch before checking exceptions. Tracing prints `<reference>`, since `String()` throws on opaque Wasm objects.
- **Empty accesses touch no page** (2026-10-15). Cronet's `NewString(env, NULL, 0)` (a JNI string of no chars) read zero bytes at address 0, and the memory bounds check raised SIGSEGV, killing YouTube at launch. A zero-length access returns before any page is checked.

### Bridge and host values

- **Boundary shape** (2026-10-04). A ~3,000-native survey kept registration keys. Imports derive from descriptors (`host/natives.ts`), strings cross as externref, primitive arrays copy through linear-memory scratch (Wasm GC cannot alias guest memory), other references are opaque behind generated accessors (`host/bridge.ts`), and JNI uses Wasm references directly (`host/jni.ts`).
- **Array copies sync at the call boundary** (2026-10-12). In a native's scope host array copies sync both ways, respecting ranged views. Copies whose scope ended stay snapshots: syncing them would cost ~2,600 copies per Calculator tap and overwrite Java's newer contents.
- **Owner-backed host buffers** (`94216e14`, `40c78785`). Host ByteBuffer consumers (decoders, audio, Bitmap, AEAD, fonts) do checked owner reads/writes and commit before returning; local heap buffers stay synchronous. A synchronous view of an owner mapping in `Typeface.nativeReadTypefaces` blacked out Doodle.
- **Host-to-Java calls are prepared once** (2026-10-08). Per `MethodInfo` (weak keys; each hit rechecks declaring class and key, since rebinding rewrites descriptors in place). Trivial body per batch: `call` 42.61→19.96 ms Node, 32.80→19.75 Chrome; `invoke` similar.
- **Facades share host prototypes** (2026-10-08). Reading a Java class from a facade's JS constructor (`constructor.$i`) broke unboxing (Lichess KeyMint lost its algorithm) and sent every exception as `java/lang/Throwable`. Use `jentry.classNameOf`; Throwable facades answer `$javaClass`.
- **Handle lifetimes** (2026-10-05). Release deleted by `deref`'s host view, not the stored Wasm array, leaving stale handles; a pending `callFn` leaked its return/throw global and is cleared only while its completion token owns it.
- **Bridge scope cost** (2026-10-05). FitNotes boundary self samples: 18,988 µs (scope begin/end 7,270, array copies 5,624). Skipping empty-scope cleanup gained nothing measurable.
- **Vsync data reads are scoped** (2026-10-13, corrected 2026-10-14, plan 1.3). `fill()` in `deliverVsync` reads `frameTimelines` through a facade, outside any native when the looper was idle in `pollOnce`'s wait. Only host-spec getters were scoped, so the facade read pushed an undo closure and a `live` entry with no scope to run them: in a dragged Komikku session, 1,186 unscoped reads pushed 1,156 closures, about one a frame. They landed in the arrays `hold()` swaps in and were dropped when the native resumed, so nothing accumulated, but the `live` entries could hand a later read in that window a stale copy. A facade read now opens a scope when none is open.
- **One owner per native** (2026-10-05). A stale `initWait(I)V` in `classes.ts` overwrote the scheduler's `initWait(I)I` after a module-order change and trapped `Th.initRace`; the scheduler owns it.

### Turns, yields and wakes

- **Yield channel and turn start** (2026-09-28..10-03). Wakes and `Thread.yield` use a `MessageChannel`, avoiding the 1–4 ms nested-`setTimeout` clamp. Main-thread back-edge yields resume first, and the 8 ms gap counts from the turn's start (`032c41c5`): Mihon warm theme taps 112 → 100 / 97 → 89 ms. Pointer events dispatch on arrival (Calculator tap p50 ~70 → 55 ms).
- **Woken threads first** (2026-10-02). `LockSupport` wakes rode the yield batch behind every native thread's slice (TikTok main's FutureTask wait 3.19 s, others 24–28 s). Woken threads and the main looper go first, one yielded thread per turn: 0.67–0.76 s and 5.7–8.0 s. Rejected: batching behind woken threads alone (no change); 4 ms turns (halved waits); a background share across throttled threads (wedged steps stayed 75–84% native).
- **Background slices are short** (2026-10-14, plans 4.1 and 4.2). In every gated window of cold and warm trio timelines the longest uninterrupted background-strand slice was 0.1–9.7 ms (above 5 ms only in Komikku's cold `next` and Calculator's cold `plus`), while main slices ran to 20 ms of app work. Time-bounded preemption of background strands, and background task priority on top of it, have little to recover; 4 ms turns and priority inheritance were rejected earlier.
- **Rejected: a turn clock from the task's start** (2026-10-14, plan 1.1). The turn clock starts at a turn's first `turnElapsed` ask. Starting it when the macrotask begins (the yield channel's handler, each host rAF, each served call), so a turn that ran host work before its first ask yields sooner, traded warm for cold over eight warm and six cold alternating Komikku `--gpu` pairs. Cold sums of tap medians were 494 vs 523 ms (`start` 86 vs 111). Warm `start` was 108 vs 92 ms: without the change it split between 55–70 and 113–121 ms, and with it stayed at 100–124. Scroll was unchanged (p95 17.5–17.6 ms both ways). Warm `next` failed its known race in 2 of 8 runs without the change and 0 of 8 with it, which is not significant at this count. The plan's other half, background strands yielding near the predicted rAF, has nothing to recover in these scrolls: of the intervals over 25 ms, one was main-thread Java (two stretches of about 11.5 ms) and one was a late host rAF with the Worker idle.
- **Rejected: longer back-edge gaps** (2026-10-01). Extending the 50 ms back-edge gap to insets/layout gave `dark` 144/269 ms outliers vs ≤83: input waits for the next yield.
- **Link admission at turn budget** (2026-10-07). Lazy links yield once the turn budget is due: TikTok's largest renderer task 21,604 → 2,341 ms in failing diagnostic runs, while data modules grew 41,930 → 69,802 KB. One compile unit or GC can still exceed it.
- **A Worker's timer due on arrival** (2026-10-15). Under a controlled clock a Worker's timers live on the page's clock (`src/shell/clock-port.ts`). One posted before the Worker heard of an advance past its time (a looper's wake-up for a message due at 5,340 ms, reaching the page at 5,388.9) was filed at the current time and ran only at the next advance. A settle stepping a frame waited on that Worker's looper, which waited on the timer: CalculatorM `launch` (1 in 5–12 launches) and FitNotes `unit-metric` held 50 s with the probe's job queued behind due messages. A timer due when it arrives now runs at once, in an advance to the current time: 25 of 25 launches, then CalculatorM 12 of 12 and FitNotes 4 of 4. It fires about 3 times in a FitNotes run.
- **Host timer overflow**. Host timers take a signed 32-bit delay; Adjust's 30-day `ask_in` wrapped, re-signing every ~300 ms. Guest delays are capped.

### Looper and vsync

- **Composite and frame close wait for nothing** (2026-10-14, plans 1.2 and 1.4). In cold Komikku timelines `queueBuffer` to composite start was 0 ms at p50 and at most 0.2 ms, and draw end to frame end the same, so compositing at latch and completing the frame close as a host continuation have nothing to recover. Vsync to `doFrame` was 0.1 ms at p95, and input reached dispatch within 0.2 ms. Scrolling FitNotes and Komikku, `doFrame` p95 was 2.5–3.6 ms, draw at most 0.9 ms and composite at most 0.5 ms, so Stage 7's layer cache, damage replay and lighter recording have no frame cost to remove in the gate apps; draw time includes any filter readback (7.4).
- **Frames at a frozen controlled clock** (2026-10-15). CalculatorM timed out about 1 in 6–8 quick-gate runs (`7`, `plus`, `7b` or `eq`), drawing 1,000–2,800 frames at one guest time. The host frame loop delivered a vsync at every host refresh while the controlled clock stood still, and time-driven animations asked for the next frame forever: RippleDrawable's background fade (a ValueAnimator invalidates on every update, value changed or not), re-recording the ripple host, the key and the display (26 ops a frame), and the ripple's render-thread animators, whose exit needed ~300 ms more. Under SwiftShader that redraw buried the GPU process and the page thread (one readback 17.9 s, one 64 ms advance 58 s), so the settle could not advance the clock the animation waited for. Whether the settle had stopped advancing when the ripple started decided the run, which is why timing shifts moved the failure rate. A request for the next vsync made inside a frame (from the dispatch until the looper next waits) is now answered only once time has passed that frame, as a display's vsyncs are a period apart; a renderer's next-vsync hook waits for a vsync later than the latest, stepped or not (hwui's `canDrawThisFrame`); the settle steps a pending frame itself with `testFrame`, past its 2 s bound too. A tap held at a frozen clock now draws 3 frames instead of ~30/s; CalculatorM passed 12 consecutive runs (11.6–15.9 s), FitNotes 13 of 13 interleaved with the starting commit (36–44 s against 46–63 s), and a quick gate took 180 s against 214–230 s. Stepping past the bound also waited on a Worker's late timer (**A Worker's timer due on arrival**). GMS's idle pid 1003 missing the 5 s diagnostic deadline during a game step (FlappyBird, Doodle Jump) reproduces at the starting commit under load. A real clock is unaffected, since its time always moves between vsyncs.
- **Looper polls on the immediate path** (2026-10-09). A Wasm override always awaited `NativeLooper.pollOnce` (5,409 Promise-returning calls per 15 s Mihon scroll, 2,068 zero-timeout with nothing to do). It is removed and a pending wake is consumed immediately when no callbacks are queued: suspending-import entries 6,504/6,667 → 3,032/3,172; presentation rates overlap.
- **Virtual-vsync phase** (2026-10-09). The pacer dropped rAF callbacks >0.75 ms early (21 of 874). `recordVsync` rounds to the nearest virtual period, skips missed periods and re-anchors to the host timestamp after a miss: synthetic 60 Hz with 1.8 ms jitter 559 → 600/600; Mihon scroll 55.13/55.26 → 56.53/56.73 presentations/s; replay 853/872 → 872/872. Rejected: tolerance only (catch-up frames); `max(time, deadline) + interval` (no fractional cadence at 90/120, 90/144, 120/144 Hz).
- **Virtual vsync from a faster host** (2026-10-13). A process Worker's animation frames come at 120 Hz on a 60 Hz virtual display, so every other host frame lands on the half-period boundary and jitter decided which frame was the vsync: of about 490 virtual intervals in a Komikku scroll, 26 were 8 ms and 19 were 26 ms (presentation p95 25 ms with doFrame p95 3 ms and no busy vsyncs). A frame is now at most half a host period early, the period being the mean of recent rAF spacings without skipped frames (a lower quartile collapsed under jitter), and an accepted frame pulls the phase an eighth toward itself. Synthetic 120 Hz/60 Hz with uniform ±1.5–3 ms jitter: 69–142 skipped vsyncs per 600 → 0 up to ±2.5 ms (24 → 2 at ±3 ms with the phase pull). Warm Komikku, 3 alternating pairs: scroll p95 25.3 → 19.3 ms, p99 27.6 → 21.8, intervals over 25 ms 18 → 2; transition p95 21–23 → 18–19 ms; Worker-side present p95 25.5 → 18.7 ms. Tap first-response times overlap.

### Caller stacks and engine limits

- **Caller frames across JSPI stacks** (2026-10-05..10). Each `callFn` enters a fresh engine stack, so caller lookup saw only the physical stack (null `MethodHandles.lookup` caller). World keeps a strand-owned chain of lazy caller Errors split by a named marker, captured only when a host call suspends, skipped for host-only entries at the scheduler root (~33 ms in FitNotes; frame depth is no guard), and shared within one Suspending import's synchronous work (captures 21,287 → 4,411; latency unchanged). Outer frames still vanish after an explicit native wait, and nested callbacks lose outer ancestry.
- **Engine support** (2026-10-07). Chrome 153 and WebKit 26.5 support GC, exnref, tail calls, extended constants, js-string builtins and JSPI; both reject suspension through a JS frame. WebKit rejects multi-memory, memory64 and relaxed SIMD, and fails suspension after a cross-module tail call (16/16; Chromium 64/64 pass); the guarded tier that relied on them was removed on 2026-10-13 and the compiler emits no tail calls ([platform status](../research/wasm/platform-status.md)). WebKit stack lines are `function@URL` with no Wasm byte offsets, so compiled PCs are -1 there (`39d01a2c`).

## Processes and system services

### Workers and the system_server page

- **App IPC is off the tap path** (2026-10-14, plans 0.4 and 6.2–6.4). `scripts/lat/census.ts` over cold and warm trio timelines: the page started serving each Worker call within 0.3 ms (6.4's condition is a p95 over 8 ms), and the main thread waited at most 7.5 ms a window on broker calls. Komikku's `more` makes 194 SQLite calls (14–20 ms of waiting), all from background threads; FitNotes' windows make 284, 0.4–0.6 ms of them on the main thread, so a batched SQLite protocol would not shorten a tap. The largest main-thread wait is FitNotes `start`: 34 `LIST_config` calls (`DeviceConfig` properties; ART caches them through a generation tracker the provider's `call` does not return) costing 5.4 ms cold and 2.4 ms warm over a 48-entry table. Settings gets took under 0.5 ms a window, and no binder transaction (`transact`) landed in one, so neither a Settings cache nor a binder pool (6.3) has a tap to shorten.
- **Every app process in a Worker** (2026-10-11). The page realm is system_server only. Workers reach the page through the broker for MediaPlayer (a page media element), system activities' browser UI, localStorage (a written-through replica) and WebView cookies (own jar). The page owns WebView iframes and passes each renderer `MessagePort` through the app Worker's bootstrap.
- **system_server boots real Java** (2026-10-05). `src/shell/system-boot.ts` runs bundled `ActivityThread.systemMain()` (uid 1000, 64-bit) on the main queue before publishing `android/system_server`. With no bound app it gets no `bindApplication` font map, so boot registers `hostWindowManager` and calls `loadSystemFontMap` itself (their absence broke a FitNotes toast).
- **Page/Worker ownership** (2026-10-05). The page owns each process's canvas, input binding, frame requests and control proxy, and disposes them on failure, death or pagehide. It imports only a control method allowlist: evaluating Worker JVM/rendering modules in the page threw `OffscreenCanvas is not defined`.
- **Bootstrap ordering** (2026-10-05). `connectProcess()` runs before `bootApk` registers the PID; eager port acquisition raised `DeadObjectException: Process is dead: 1000` (Komikku), so ports open lazily. A MessagePort does not detect a dead peer: the broker closes its owners on death. Bootstrap passes archive ports, not live `PackedArchive`s (`4f090a6f`).
- **Readiness is not health** (2026-10-05). A Worker without its path binding reached ready, then hit `SQLITE_CANTOPEN` in WorkManager and drew nothing. A control reply does not prove Java queue liveness, so `wd.alive(ms)` checks the root and every shown Worker's main queue.
- **Per-process memory** (2026-09-30). Calculator M's three processes retained 2.1 GB, mostly 256 MiB native-core Wasm memory and 128 MiB CanvasKit heap each; untouched Wasm pages are not resident.
- **Deferred: cross-origin isolation** (2026-10-11). Nothing needs `SharedArrayBuffer` (mappings use leases, audio crosses the media port); isolation would constrain cross-origin WebView frames.
- **Reverted: starting Play services earlier** (2026-10-10). Mihon idled ~2 s on a synchronous GMS provider query while the GMS Worker booted. Starting GMS at process registration left Mihon unchanged (that boot waits on the page) and cost Calculator up to ~70 ms.

### Binder, Parcels and Intents

- **Hosted services cross a parcel** (2026-09-30..10-02). TS AIDL Stubs (`hostStub`) act as system_server (uid 1000). Apps get BinderProxies and `execTransact` runs over parcel copies, so >200 KB throws TransactionTooLargeException (microG's GET_META_DATA provider query: 1.4 MB). Cost (bun): trivial call +6 µs, relayout +58 µs; FitNotes makes ~140 calls to first frame.
- **No synchronous binder call across the client's own queue** (2026-10-03/04). startActivity/finishActivity awaited a pause the caller's parked main thread had to run (deadlock when onCreate starts an activity). ATM returns once the start is under way, as ActivityStarter does.
- **Parcels and Intents** (2026-09-28..10-05). A ParcelFileDescriptor crosses as bytes plus a writer binder written back on last close, so imported parcels must be recycled. Bundles stay parcelled until read, so the broker routes without the sender's classes; keys iterate by hash (androidx.startup order follows). An `Account[]` sent as object array failed AndroidX `getParcelableArray`. IntentFilter.match takes 0.1-0.4 ms for YouTube's ~90 filters, 16-20 ms on first build.
- **Framework data and providers** (2026-10-03/04). Data classes framework callers cast must be AOSP's: a TS RunningAppProcessInfo failed Firebase's cast and aborted bindApplication (Mihon's Injekt error). Providers install by descending initOrder; a local holder needs `mLocal`, or ActivityThread waits 20 s.
- **Worker Binder lifetime** (`5ae61c70`). Death watches go through the process owner; only tokens cross, recipients stay in their JVM, and a late ack/unlink/death cannot resurrect a removed one.

### Cross-process storage ownership

- **Worker store contract** (2026-10-05). Canonical stores (SQLite, cookies, clipboard, settings, accounts, preferences, notifications, private files, exit history...) stay with the page. Worker commands bind the registered PID, the owner resolves UID (supplied identity is ignored), and acknowledged granular mutations, never whole-file writeback, land before main-looper callbacks. Recurring bugs: a returned Promise read as a value, and a port opened before PID registration.
- **Per-store authority** (2026-10-05). Private preferences need same UID or system. PendingIntents match with Android's `Intent.filterEquals` in the owner; only serialized Intents cross. Alarms stay in the broker and can fire into a replacement. Account visibility grants identity, not token access; a password change invalidates tokens.
- **SQLite process service** (2026-10-05). Connections share one canonical sql.js file so connection-local TEMP/PRAGMA/transaction state survives. Statement tokens retire at finalize dispatch (SQLite destroys the statement even if finalize errors); a `close_v2` zombie keeps its file owner until its last statement finalizes.
- **Service starts and location** (2026-10-05). Service lifecycle steps await owner acks; no ServiceRecords or callbacks cross. Location subscriptions carry a generation token, so stale events miss a replacement listener.

### Descriptors and shared memory

- **Canonical regular-file descriptors** (2026-10-05). ChatGPT's DataStore `…json.tmp` ENOENT had a split owner: canonical `File.mkdir` succeeded but Worker-local `open(O_CREAT)` saw no parent. `src/shell/file-descriptors.ts` owns regular files behind PID-bound commands that Worker libcore and ARM calls await; `dup` shares offset and flags, CLOEXEC is per descriptor. Pipes/sockets are unsupported.
- **Path natives follow the owning realm** (2026-10-12). Mihon's DataStore failed every rename: a Worker's `UnixNativeDispatcher` path calls read its own VFS while app data lives in the broker's. They and `java.io.File`'s mtime/permission natives now go to the owner, as `Os` did.
- **Descriptor rights in Binder** (2026-10-05..07). A transaction sends a one-shot token; the broker installs a CLOEXEC receiver reference sharing backing and offset. Clipboard and notifications keep rights in a broker `StoredParcel` that outlives the sender and dups into each reader. PendingIntent creation rejects descriptor-bearing Intents, as Android does. Shared ashmem rights fail.
- **Live mapping leases** (`fb4867ae`, `e2655f19`, `ab801620`). File-range leases outlive the descriptor and back SharedMemory, `Os.mmap` and FileChannel mappings, sharing pages across close/unlink/truncate; mutation epochs close a release-before-flush hole. Raw JNI addresses, synchronous byte views, shared atomics and native MAP_SHARED (ENOTSUP) stay unsupported.
- **`fixupAppDir`** (2026-10-05). Follows Android 15 vold, repairing only existing components (package UID, category GID, 02770). No quota or ACLs; no app run showed a call.

### Services and process death

- **System work outlives app processes** (2026-10-02). Broadcast resolution, PendingIntent matching, alarms and death unbinds ran on app JVMs or guest timers that a dead process never resumes. The broker owns them (`setSystemTimeout`); a separate system realm was rejected then at 0.6-0.7 s, ~75 MiB.
- **Service restarts follow ActiveServices** (2026-10-02/03). Broker ServiceRecords own starts and bindings. After a host dies, unfinished starts redeliver (≤3), sticky ones return with a null intent, auto-create bindings rebind; delays 1 s, x4 within a minute. Repeat crashes restart only foreground starts, up to 16. Not modelled: procIsBoundForeground, the bad-process mark.
- **Service lifetime after onDestroy** (2026-09-30). onDestroy posts runnables whose LifecycleRegistry holds the service weakly; dropping it on return broke ChatGPT. A main-queue job holds it, as CLEAN_UP_CONTEXT does (3/6 → 0/8).
- **Bound processes inherit priority** (2026-10-02). DoodleJump deadlocks unless billing binds before its game loop, but the service booted at background priority (bind 7-8 s). A process now inherits a waiting client's priority and scheduling group (OomAdjuster): bind 0.8-0.9 s.
- **Process death and crashes** (2026-10-02..05). Exit paths and SIGKILL never return (`src/process-exit.ts`). The broker's `fail(pid, message, death)` fires death recipients, throws DeadObjectException to callers and cancels bindings and activities. Oneway stubs follow execTransact (RuntimeException warns, Error aborts). AMS shows a dialog only on a repeat within MIN_CRASH_INTERVAL; exits persist per package (16).
- **Terminal classification** (2026-10-05). Dead rows carry `terminal: {expected, cause, record}`; only EXIT_SELF status 0 or USER_REQUESTED without a crash is expected (`NOT_CRASHED` alone is not).

### Activities, windows and input

- **ATM fidelity** (2026-09-26..10-04). Launch modes, task flags, Back and home follow ActivityStarter. Clients hold BinderProxy ATM tokens (local Binders made `recreate` a no-op). A second pause waits on the first; a start ignores a record a finish destroyed (TikTok's splash). `wd.back()` returns a Promise settled by the owner's ack.
- **System windows and input routing** (2026-10-05). The root canvas composites transparently above app canvases. Page input applies WindowManager focus and touch-modal/`NOT_TOUCHABLE` rules before routing to a Worker; navigation keys go to the root while a root window has focus.
- **IME across the Worker boundary** (2026-10-05). The page owns a hidden text control per canvas; the Worker keeps the InputConnection. Commands carry an editor generation rechecked on the main queue. Typing `60` mirrored `6060` because an async partial ExtractedText landed on the optimistic update; partials now apply to a last-reported baseline. Root IME shares the path.
- **A physical rotation reaches the app** (2026-10-15). `wd.configuration` asked only the page realm's top activity and turned only the page's display, so a Worker app filling the display kept its orientation while the page's turned. ActivityStack now sends the rotation to the process whose activity fills the display, as DisplayRotation resolves the sensor's rotation against that activity's request, behind queued activity changes. That process keeps a fixed, nosensor or locked request and otherwise turns; the page follows the turn it reports. FitNotes (unspecified) turns with the page; Flappy Bird (portrait) keeps both. The rotation is now device-wide sensor state, as DisplayRotation reads the sensor's proposed rotation: ActivityStack keeps the last one (the configured screen's until then), and a process whose activity comes to fill the display resolves it against its own top activity's request by the same rule, as updateOrientation does when the orientation source changes, unawaited so the queue never waits on the page's main loop. The page's `ownLandscape` is gone, so its own fixed request holds when it fills the display again. With microG's launcher activity in its own Worker over FitNotes, microG's process, left in portrait, now turns landscape when it fills the display again after a rotation made with FitNotes in front, and FitNotes turns back to portrait after one made with microG in front; at fbdc6a0ab both kept their old orientation. Flappy Bird (portrait) keeps portrait throughout while microG takes the sensor's landscape. Each realm now keeps that state too, from its bootstrap and from every rotation, which ActivityStack records in all processes, and its atm resolves by the same rule whenever an activity starts, resumes or changes its request: the request it resolves is that of the top activity that is visible and either specifies an orientation or occludes, as WindowContainer.getOrientation takes it. So a new Worker launches its activity in the resolved rotation, and a finished activity's orientation gives way to the one it reveals. FitNotes rotated landscape turns portrait for TrainingLogActivity (portrait) and back to landscape for the exercise list a back returns to; at a6f01af84 the atm turned only for a fixed request, so by its code the list kept portrait. Nosensor takes the natural rotation, as rotationForOrientation gives it; locked, and a realm with no activity that sets the orientation, keep the display as it is. Limits: a translucent process shown over the one that fills the display resolves only its own activities, so it keeps its orientation when that one turns, where AOSP gives every window the display's rotation; no flow has shown one across a rotation. A behind request (3) resolves as the sensor's rotation, where getOrientation takes the orientation of the activity beneath it.
- **Multi-pointer touch** (2026-10-15). The shell dispatched one pointer per canvas through `MotionEvent.obtain(JJIFFI)`, so no pinch or rotation reached an app. `pointer-input.ts` now keeps every browser pointer of a gesture and numbers them as InputReader does (lowest free id, listed in id order): the first press is DOWN, later ones POINTER_DOWN with their index, POINTER_UP still carries the lifting pointer and the last lift is UP; a `pointercancel` cancels the whole gesture, and the pointers still down are ignored until they lift. `wm.dispatchTouch` builds every event with the PointerProperties/PointerCoords `obtain`, with fresh objects per event because another dispatch can run while one waits on a construction. The tool type is now FINGER (UNKNOWN before); pressure and size stay 1. The window under the first pointer takes the whole gesture; split touch across windows is not modeled. Chrome runs a microtask checkpoint after each browser-dispatched listener, so a microtask flush still sent one MOVE per finger (CDP: 37 MOVEs for 24 two-finger steps). Several fingers' moves now wait for `drainPointers` in the animation frame, which Chrome runs right after the frame's rAF-aligned input (24 for 24); a lone pointer still dispatches each move at once. A mouse or pen press with Alt held adds a second finger mirrored through the display's centre, as the emulator's pinch does. Process canvases lacked `touch-action: none` (only `#screen` had it), so Chrome took a real two-finger touch as its own pinch-zoom: the app got CANCEL after three MOVEs, and the page itself zoomed, which looked like the map zooming further after each later gesture. In Google Maps a CDP two-finger spread zooms in, an Alt-held drag toward the centre zooms out, a two-finger turn rotates the map (the compass appears) and a one-finger drag pans, with no new missing APIs or uncaught errors. `lat` kept every gated tap under 150 ms (slowest Komikku `appearance`, 144 ms).
- **Trackpad pinch** (2026-10-15). Chrome sends a trackpad pinch, like Ctrl + a mouse wheel, as `wheel` events with `ctrlKey` set and deltaY = -100 ln(scale): a synthesized 3× touchpad pinch arrived as 45 events summing to -109.86. The shell treated every wheel burst as a one-finger drag, so a pinch scrolled the app. A Ctrl burst now drives two fingers centred on the cursor along the display's longer side. They go down (DOWN, POINTER_DOWN) once each finger's travel passes the touch slop and lift (POINTER_UP 0, UP) when the stream idles; a burst that gains or drops Ctrl lifts the gesture it drove. They start two `config_minScalingSpan` apart (27 mm, 510 px at 480 dpi) and come no closer than one, since ScaleGestureDetector ends a scale below it. One burst therefore zooms out at most 2×, while zooming in may carry the fingers off the screen, as the scroll finger can. Moving the fingers straight to the accumulated scale failed in Google Maps. With one or two MOVEs after POINTER_DOWN it scaled nothing, and neither did a CDP two-finger touch with two MOVEs; with three it scaled. After one of those gestures a dropped pin appeared at the first finger's press point. Scale detectors begin on the MOVE that crosses their slop and measure from that span, so the first MOVE now only crosses the slop and each later frame covers half the remaining ratio; a wheel notch (2.7×) arrives as about 14 MOVEs. In Maps, Ctrl+wheel notches and synthesized touchpad pinches (3×, 2×, 0.6×) zoom in and out about the cursor and a plain wheel still pans. The process reported no missing APIs, native requirements or draw errors. Open: the fingers press wherever the span reaches. In portrait Maps (1080×2160, `maps-wasm`), both fingers land on the map only for a cursor in a band about 60 px tall between the chip row and the sheet. Elsewhere the first finger presses the search bar or a chip, and split touch hands the map one pointer, so the pinch scales nothing.

## Java and framework semantics

### Libcore natives and contracts

- **TS copies kept breaking documented contracts** (2026-09-26..10-03). Most pre-migration failures were a TS copy missing one: copied `subList`/map views (Compose, Glide), an `LruCache` without `entryRemoved` (SQLCipher close BUSY), TextView skipping virtual `getCompoundPadding*`. `bun run bypass` and `desccheck` guard what TS still implements; TS exchanges collections through their Java API (`jcoll.ts`), where comparators and iteration can suspend.
- **Semantics the intrinsics and JNI must keep** (2026-09-27..10-01). Android's `u_islower` is category Ll, so `ª` is not lower case (OpenJDK says it is); `compiler/chars.ts` must follow ICU. `StrictMath`'s fdlibm port (`fdlibm.ts`) is bit-exact on 435 AVD probes. JNI strings use ART's modified UTF-8: NUL as two bytes, a valid surrogate pair as one four-byte sequence.
- **Regex over a JS RegExp translation** (2026-10-01). Pattern/Matcher run over `PatternNative`/`MatcherNative` in `jregex.ts`. `.`, `^` and `$` use ICU's line terminators and UNIX_LINES; Java's `\x{h}` is JS's `\u{h}`; under the `v` flag a property escape in a class set needs its braces. hitEnd/requireEnd follow ICU's RegexMatcher (kept across `find()`), computed only when read by derived patterns consuming an appended sentinel.
- **Charsets are ICU's** (2026-09-29..10-01). UTF-32 encodes LE after `FF FE 00 00` and decodes BE unmarked. After malformed input a decoder sits past it, an encoder before it; an unmapped single-byte-table byte is unmappable, not malformed (`src/jvm/charset.ts`). Open device mismatch: `new String({0xc3}, "UTF-16")` is "" on the device, U+FFFD here.
- **zlib natives run pako at z_stream level** (2026-10-01). okio and GZIP trailers read unconsumed input back through `getRemaining`, which fflate cannot report. pako's `updatewindow` skips resizing after `inflateReset`, so a reused Inflater failed "invalid distance too far back"; the reset native sizes the window.
- **Bootstrap order and references** (2026-10-07/12). FileSystems init reached VMClassLoader, whose JarFiles re-entered the incomplete default filesystem; `preloaded-classes` initializes VMClassLoader first, as `66e6b7d2` does in the async harness bootstrap. core-oj's `Reference.enqueue()` only queues and keeps the referent.

### Files, storage and SQLite

- **Files and descriptors on the VFS** (2026-09-28..10-01). symlink/link fail EPERM, readlink EINVAL; rename under a non-directory parent fails ENOENT/ENOTDIR. ProfileInstaller needs installd's `/data/misc/profiles/cur/0/<pkg>/` and catches IOException, not ErrnoException. `jniCreateFileDescriptor` goes through `FileDescriptor.setInt$`, which records append; writable provider descriptors need a backing file; a length-0 asset descriptor made AssetFileDescriptor drop every write.
- **Large files on OPFS**. Files past 4 MiB move to OPFS, one worker holding sync handles and running requests in order; IndexedDB keeps `{id, size}`. All opens of a path share one `VfsFile`, and shared writes refresh open views (Organic Maps' range downloads overwrote each other; a short native view failed Monaco's BLAKE3). SQLite and preferences stay in memory for synchronous readers. Genshin's full download exceeds the 4.7 GB quota.
- **Private file flush order** (2026-09-27). `PrivateFiles.flush` committed a spilled file's record before its bytes, the likely cause of Cut the Rope failing `Assembly::Load("mscorlib.dll")` after an interrupted first boot; bytes now land first.
- **SQLite over sql.js** (2026-10-05/06). One sqlite3 handle per connection keeps its TEMP state (sql.js export reopens a connection, destroying TEMP objects). Snapshots follow transactions through a nonblocking PASSIVE WAL checkpoint, wait for autocommit on every connection, and one staleness rule stops a pending one overwriting a restored database. The vendor build binds real int64 (unsafe numbers reread as BigInt). Open+close of 40 MB 7.7 → 1.4 ms. Error codes and collator strengths are unverified.

### Networking, crypto and TLS

- **HttpURLConnection through the proxy** (2026-09-27..10-03). Requests use the proxy's `manual=1` mode and follow redirects hop by hop for CookieHandler; redirect URIs escape `|`, `{` and bare `%` as OkHttp's `HttpUrl.uri()` does. Akamai rejects requests lacking `Sec-Fetch-*` or browser Accept-Encoding, or with headless `sec-ch-*`; a service worker cannot see forbidden headers it forwards, so it rebuilds them from `request.mode`/`destination`.
- **JCA and TLS** (2026-10-01). JCA runs from libcore over conscrypt NativeCrypto natives; async WebCrypto cannot back it. AndroidKeyStore uses a keystore2 service with a software KeyMint. TLS ends at the relay, whose CA and per-host leaf pass hostname checks; certificate pinning fails.
- **Crypto costs** (2026-10-01). SHA-256 in JS tops out at 3.1 ms/MB, so digests ≥ 64 KB use `crypto.subtle.digest` (1 MB 3.3 → 0.46 ms). ECDSA P-256 verify 467 → 310–322 µs (Barrett reduction, point tables). Rejected: Int32/Float64 limbs, Lehmer inversion, per-verify WebCrypto (a suspension each).

### WebView

- **Renderer origins** (2026-10-02). ## Rendering and text

### Display lists and replay

- **hwui replay keeps display lists** (2026-10-02). `hwui.ts`'s RenderProxy replays the root RenderNode into the surface buffer at `nSyncAndDrawFrame`, replacing the migration plan's forced `drawSoftware` on purpose: unchanged views keep their display lists and re-run no `onDraw`. Nested nodes and video are read at replay.
- **Recorder costs** (2026-10-02/03, `4ae6a100`). The recorder keeps its own affine and save stack; path ops are record-only (micro 487 → 115 ms; Mihon scroll flat at ~700 recorded ops/s, Java draw code dominating). RenderNode transforms compose into the retained matrix instead of building 13 Float64Arrays (100k updates 292.8 → 2.3 ms). A display-list rewrite is unproven; per-command Wasm adds crossings, so it needs a batched boundary.
- **One render-thread draw per frame** (2026-10-03, `bdb6be6a`). RT animators drew via `onNextVsync` before `doFrame`, so a vsync could composite twice (Calculator 291 draws / 126 main frames); the RT draw now runs behind `doFrame` and is skipped after a draw: 2.09 → 1.19 draws per main frame.

- **Native handle lifetimes** (2026-10-05). - **A native's own store claims** (2026-10-15). - **A recording reaches its whole target** (2026-10-15). A display list records over a one-pixel mirror, so geometry taken from the mirror's canvas confined a BitmapShader fill, `drawPaint` and an inverse path or clip to a one-pixel strip at replay: YouTube's thumbnails and avatars stayed gray. A recording's target bounds are now the plane (±1e5), and a bitmap shader's pattern, which covers its target, is made at replay as a RuntimeShader's is.

- **Layers, filters and clips**. `saveLayer`, compositing layers and `setLayerType` share one offscreen path (a plain save broke Lottie's DST_IN/OUT masks); offscreen bitmaps present atomically. ## Native code, media and transport

### CPU cores and JIT

- **Interpreter/JIT agreement**. A region discovery budget let a branch enter with stale carry (Lichess P-384 TLS, JIT only); JIT exits omitted a pending compare's flags, so a resumed handler saw stale NZCV.
- **JIT regions**. Forward branches use direct `br`; a region spills at every entry/exit (Among Us ~13.8M entries/s) and never spans a guest page. Compiling counted `fallback`s cut exits 774k/s → 71k/s; 37% of memory accesses take plane translation. Rejected: dead flags at A64 call/return exits (no gain, breaks interpreter equality); scalar FP in cached v128 locals (within ±5%).
- **ARM32 JIT coverage** (2026-09-27). VFP, vldm/vstm, exclusives, barriers and NEON exited to the interpreter, spilling every register; `jitneon.zig` and `jit32thumb.zig` compile them (Thumb loop 20.7 → 1.0 ns/instr; Cut the Rope's top fallback sites 35.4M → 1.6M exits per 50 s). Diffing 48,280 IT blocks against the interpreter found stray Wasm stack pushes (invalid only where a block lacks a terminal branch).
- **Genshin JIT profile** (M4 Pro). ~290M guest instr/s; 14.8M region entries/s of ~20 instructions, transitions ~30% (21 ns each), 103 Wasm bytes per guest instruction. Rejected: regions taking blocks from three other pages (half the transitions, but 166M instr/s from duplicated code). In-core `clock_gettime` cut 45K exits/s, no fps change. 60 fps needs ~2.3×.
- **Writes into code and exclusives**. JNI array copies into code left stale decoded instructions; they use checked helpers (`e3b51392`). A64 STXR/STLXR/STXP wrote with no reservation held; they now fail without writing (`924414a3`).

### Guest memory and mappings

- **Shared guest memory in 64 MiB steps** (2026-10-02). V8 counts a Wasm memory's committed bytes toward the isolate heap limit, shared or not (two grown 4 GiB memories: 23.7 vs 0.27 s GC). Committing the ABI's size up front (4 GiB arm64, 2 GiB ARM32), since growth detached direct buffers' views, kept incremental marking running: Mihon taps spent 10.4-11.7 of ~23 s in GC, TikTok 52%. Guest memory is now shared (no cross-origin isolation needed in Chrome), grows in 64 MiB steps without detaching, and `mem_grown` refreshes cached views: TikTok GC 2.8%, `--live` failures 20 → 1. TextDecoder, `getRandomValues`, Blob, ImageData, `WebSocket.send` and structured clone reject shared views. Rejected: lazy commit (non-movable arrays pin memory), resizable buffers (Web APIs reject views; reads 4-13× slower), per-realm isolates, a 3 GiB reservation (wedged). In isolation 64 MiB steps did not slow allocation; some single large grows did (206-219 vs 127-137 ms).
- **Address planes** (2026-10-02..03). The core's 32-bit pc needs plane 0, so executable and low-hinted mappings stay there. Planes move across awaits: `anchor(p)` keeps a guest address, and `scripts/generators.ts` rejects load-time PTR/KLAYOUT/MEM_SIZE reads (msghdr offsets computed while PTR was 4 wedged YouTube's CronetNet). Writable limits commit on touch, bypassed by raw typed-array views. Rejected: per-2-MiB plane units (host ops holding an offset across an await wrote the old place; Unity 0 vs 19.5 fps).
- **File mappings** (2026-10-02..05). `MAP_SHARED` file mappings are copies; msync, munmap, reads and new mappings carry changed bytes to the file and every mapping (Realm read stale arrays), and flushing stops at the file length (`SharedMaps.flush` grew a 3-byte file to 8). A bulk get over an external mapping waits for its pages (Normalizer's NFC data read zeros).
- **Cross-process `MAP_SHARED` needs one owner** (2026-10-05). Each process's memory (`core.ts`) holds all its pages (`mem.zig`) for Java and JIT accesses; sharing it would alias fixed-address MMU/core state, and RPC flush/diff is insufficient. A design must keep control state private and route all accesses to one owner. Native remote `MAP_SHARED`/ashmem stays unsupported.

### Signals, faults and threads

- **Guest faults are synchronous signals** (2026-10-02). Null, unbacked and write-protected accesses raise SIGSEGV, misalignment SIGBUS, BRK SIGTRAP, with si_addr and the kernel's frame (fpsimd_context, which Mono asserts). Bad pointers in syscalls and stub-like libc bodies answer EFAULT. SIGILL reaches a handler only for `.undefined` and optional encodings HWCAP does not advertise (OpenSSL armcap probes); `.unimplemented` stays a host fault, so crash SDKs see no fabricated signal.
- **Debuggerd and defaults** (2026-10-02). A host debuggerd handler logs the tombstone and ends the process (libunity looped on a fault 9,000 times in 3 s). Unhandled signals take signal(7)'s default. Cpus start with ART's mask (`SigBlk 0x80001204`), so SIGPIPE stays pending.
- **ucontext restored inside `Cpu.call`**. Restoring after the call let a frame whose host call completed run, then lose registers to the signal snapshot (Boehm's signals crashed Among Us); a nested-frame tripwire found it where `?jit=0` (~4M instr/s) was too slow. Futex and yield completion defer queued signals.
- **Handlers that never return** (2026-10-02). libmetasec_ov's SIGRTMAX handler restores pre-raise registers by hand; returning, it hit the sentinel with the interrupted JNI call's sp and resumed a stale frame into .bss, so anti-tamper killed TikTok. Such a return now also returns from the interrupted call, keeping the handler's mask as Linux does without sigreturn.
- **Threads and children**. A process's guest pthreads share one JS thread; sustained background work (ThreatMetrix, Adjust `libsigner`) gets about a quarter during input and presentation. Joinable pthread handles stay until join (reuse caused Lichess EDEADLK). fork, vfork and non-thread clone answer EAGAIN (no Android kernel gives ENOSYS), wait ECHILD; emulating `sh -c` would fabricate a child.

### bionic and the syscall layer

- **bionic integration** (2026-10-03; decision in `docs/architecture.md`). It needed this build's TLS/TCB offsets, `.symtab` helpers, attached host threads, the malloc dispatch table (libc also calls malloc directly), the linker's CFI shadow for `dnsOpenProxy`, and a root-owned `/dev/__properties__` with per-property serials (else localtime ran in GMT); Scudo needed core extents, CRC32 and TBI. FORTIFY aborted TikTok on `readdir(NULL)`; `pthread_getattr_np` needs the main stack in `/proc/self/maps` (YouTube). There is no libc flag: an A/B against the HLE libc needs a worktree at `33b23028` with `?libc=bionic` unset.
- **ABI slips**. `Args64` read stack arguments from plane 0 (Lichess drew no text); ARM32 mmap64 read its off64_t from the padding slot.
- **Sockets are open files** (2026-10-02..03). `OpenFile.socket` lets dup, poll, epoll and SCM_RIGHTS see one kind; sendmsg/recvmsg had been ENETUNREACH. ### EGL, GLES and WebGL

- **Synchronous WebGL queries** (2026-10-02). `getError`/`getParameter` block; the first after a present waits for the frame (24-185 ms). gles3 tracks pixel-store state (getParameter took ~770 ms of a Snapseed open), whether WebGL can have latched an error (`keepsClean`: Lichess getError 1.12-1.71 → 0.62-0.64 s per 12 s) and recorded upload levels (TikTok's per-upload `glGetError` took 5.06 s of a 10 s step). Rejected: a fence per present (100-130 ms polling, no gain).
- **Buffers, formats and surfaces**. Client-array draws read indices back via `getBufferSubData`; WebGL rejects `GL_FIXED`. ReadPixels commits only initialized rows (`8d3437e8`). `eglQuerySurface` ignored `setBuffersGeometry` and `getWidth` returned the set geometry, not the consumer default (Among Us 375×188; now 900×450 as on the API-33 emulator).
- **Current-state EGL14 natives** (2026-10-13). YouTube crashed a HandlerThread with `UnsatisfiedLinkError` for `EGL14.eglGetCurrentDisplay()`; EGL14 lacked the current-display and current-surface queries, `eglQuerySurface`, `eglBindAPI`/`eglQueryAPI`, the `long` `eglGetDisplay` and the waits, which EGL10 had. ### Media codecs

- **MediaCodec over WebCodecs** (2026-10-02). Decoders are `c2.webcodecs.<type>.decoder`, vendor and hardware-accelerated like the AVD's `c2.goldfish.*`; Exynos names were rejected because apps key quirks on them. Codec-config buffers lead the next key frame or become an audio description; input before a key frame and length-prefixed samples decode to nothing, as on devices. YouTube plays VP9 at parity.
- **A new buffer per `getBuffer`** (2026-10-15). `MediaCodec.getInputBuffer` invalidates the cached buffer at that index and `queueInputBuffer` frees it, since JMediaCodec wraps the codec's memory with `NewDirectByteBuffer` on every call, read-only for output. Returning the codec's own buffer each time left the one ExoPlayer wrote into inaccessible and failed every YouTube playback; each call now hands out a new view.

### Relay, proxy, TLS and DNS

- **UDP relay** (2026-10-02). Replies framed with the resolved address were dropped by sockets connected to the synthetic one, stalling YouTube's QUIC; replies name the dialed address. UDP connect to IPv6 is ENETUNREACH. The deployed Worker has no UDP endpoint.
- **TLS, DNS and the proxy**. Native TLS verifies against pinned AOSP roots; Java TLS ends at the relay, whose certificate lacks a public key. Guest resolvers take DNS from `LinkProperties`; port 53 goes through `/dns-query`. Bun may strip `Content-Length`, so it travels separately (AntennaPod).

### TikTok

- **The shared 4 GB cage** (2026-10-11..12). Process and compile Workers share the renderer's pointer-compression cage; TikTok's launch crashed it (`V8 javascript OOM`) at 4.1-4.4 GB committed, every realm holding a Program and World over its dexes. After `REFINE_SHARE` the e2e flow completes, but the cage still swings 3.7-4.2 GB. V8 sizes each isolate from its own allocation rate (compiling churns ~200 MB/s); allocation cuts in `lateBy`, vtable initializers and pool-stub bodies did not change the GC count.
- **Compile workers in a sandboxed frame** (2026-10-14). The crash recurred at ~105 s (cage 4.39 GB). Sampling profiles: the main process held ~1.34 GB live at 200 s (~300 MB Wasm instances/exports, ~240 MB Program/dex metadata, ~80 MB pool stubs); the app build's Worker ~330 MB live of 691 MB used. A sandboxed `srcdoc` frame (opaque origin) gets its own renderer and cage under site isolation: 4 Workers × 450 units in the page crashed, 2 in the page plus 2 in such a frame × 600 survived. Every process Worker's compile workers now start in one such frame (`host/compilehost.ts`); an opaque origin rejects a module Worker even from a blob, so a classic blob Worker `import()`s the module blob. Platform jars and builds are fetched through a port to the page's origin. TikTok's e2e flow rendered 13/13 in three runs with app-cage peaks 2.5-2.6 GB (compile host 1.9-2.1 GB); none exceeded 3.5 GB. Limits: if the host's renderer dies its workers vanish without an error, so the app keeps first-tier code and refines stop; a browser that keeps the frame in the page's process (Android Chrome, Safari) runs them in the shared cage as before (untested on WebKit).
- **Units lost across the frame** (2026-10-15, `8392a936`). A second-tier unit carried its `WebAssembly.Module`, which does not deserialize in another agent cluster. The process Worker therefore got a `messageerror` that nothing handled, and its client stayed busy after the first job. From the move into the frame until this fix, no app in site-isolated Chrome upgraded or merged a method. Google Maps launched with 27,811 first-tier units and 0 upgrades; sending the validated bytes, it adopted 562 of 566 jobs and merged 512. An unreadable message now retires the client.
- **Restored units cost Komikku's taps** (2026-10-15, open). With units adopted again, Komikku's cold `settings` tap took 100–127 ms over five `lat` runs against 65–98 ms at `cae91309`, which loses them. Both used one platform build, made from the same hot profile, on a host at load 7–13; `appearance` medians were about 155 and 125 ms. In that tap the branch compiled 2.5× the first-tier methods (175–224 against 80–93) in the same compile time and dropped a retire batch. One run with the client refusing every job matched the base (68 ms, 76 methods). The likely cost is the engine compiling merged functions on their first call in the tap, since they replaced code it had already compiled; holding waits out only the input window. Measure on an idle host before changing merge pacing. `lat`'s Mihon scenario fails at launch ("Rendered app launch unavailable") on both sides.
- **One build per app** (2026-10-14). Every process of a package built under the same key, each with a compile Worker holding a World over the app's dexes and replacing the others' build. A `webdroid:wasm-app:<key>` Web Lock now lets the first process to launch make and refine the build until its watch ends.
- **Rejected: slot-indexed pool stubs** (2026-10-14). Filling the table with `put(i, slot)` instead of exporting each stub saved ~5 MB of ~80 MB: V8's per-entry funcref cost is about that of an export.
- **First launch** (2026-10-11..12). On-demand compiling took 26-29.6 s and native crypto/bignum 27.6 s. Before unit merging, 186 mark-compacts (130 for external memory, which reached 7.7 GB) took 27 s of 130 s.
- **Swipes and the first video page** (2026-10-02..05). Wedged swipes stalled 4-17 s on the first video page's cold code or `KevaFuture.getKeva` waits while guest threads took 47-85%.
- **Video** (2026-10-02..05). ByteVC HEVC decodes 71 frames in 776-785 ms with the native JIT vs 3,913 ms without; NEON software decode runs JIT-compiled (fallbacks 0.07%) at 0.15-0.7 G instr/s, 2-5 frames/s. ### YouTube

- **Playing on the Wasm tier** (2026-10-15). After the empty-access, line-break, codec-buffer and recording fixes, the quick flow reached all 16 steps and a full capture played Big Buck Bunny and swiped Shorts with thumbnails drawn. The full lane's video-open tap is a coordinate (540,1350), which opened a Short placed first in the live results, so the player-control steps found no controls: live content, not a fault.
- **A stale long named a slot's next occupant** (2026-10-15). In about 1 of 9 walks the fullscreen step killed the app on the main thread, in the player-overlay pipeline `gvt.e` → `nvz` → `ViewStub.inflate`: twice a `TouchImageView` element's layout params lacked `layout_width` (`youtube_controls_fullscreen_button`, a literal 60dp; `youtube_controls_button_group_layout`, a dimen reference), and once `nuf.a` found no view `0x7f0b18d6` in what had been inflated. A handle's long was its slot number, and `claimHandle` ties an owned object to the first object of a class with `finalize()` that stores its long. A long kept after its object was released, or any long field holding a small number, therefore named whatever reused the slot: with generation-tagged instrumentation, a walk freed a live SurfaceTexture (its handle is stored from the host, so its own SurfaceTexture never claims it) when a holder of slot 372's previous long died. A slot freed under a live holder is reused, and the holder's next release frees the new occupant; a parse state that shared its slot with another parser's would read that parser's elements, which fits both crashes. That last link is inferred: no crash was caught with the chain logged, and instrumented runs crashed less often. Before this fix the same checks saw nothing for a native array copy stored back over newer Java writes, `applyStyle` given a non-array target or no node, `layout_width` resolving to null inside `applyStyle`, a parse state released other than by `nativeDestroyParseState`, or a Java array's handle released. A long now carries its slot's generation (1–127 above 24 slot bits, below 2^31), which each release advances, so a stale or plain long derefs, releases and claims nothing. After the fix 22 walks had no crash; 20 reached the player and passed fullscreen, and 2 lost the live search results. The SurfaceTexture's handle was never claimed, since only compiled stores claimed; see *A native's own store claims*.

## Deployment and tooling

### Production build

- **Import cycles break only in production**. Chunking reorders modules under cycles, so a top-level `extends` hit the TDZ only in the production bundle. Remove top-level uses of cyclic imports (`tests/import-cycles.test.ts` rejects them) and verify shell changes on `build:prod` under `wrangler dev`.
- **Gzip transport for WebKit** (`9e208dd2`). Bun's synthesized gzip filename/MIME made WebKit keep compressed bytes despite `Content-Encoding`; binary MIME with inline disposition decodes in both engines.
- **Deployment checks**. They skip arbitrary APKs and the UDP path; the Worker/Pages split and its same-site reason are in `docs/deployment.md`.

### Shared host and worktrees

- **Shared-host hygiene**. Other sessions' servers and runs share the host: never `pkill -f server.ts` or `pkill -f e2e`; stop your own server by port (`lsof -ti tcp:<port> | xargs kill`). Restart the dev server after changing `server.ts` or vendor files (a stale server served 404s that failed flows). Never `git stash -u` while a server runs from the tree.
- **Worktree setup**. Agent worktrees start from `main`; reset them onto the working branch while clean. Symlink `apks`, `node_modules`, `vendor/aosp`, `vendor/microg` and `conform-cache`, and give each its own port. Check that a link target does not exist first: `ln -s` into an existing symlinked directory nests a link inside the real vendor directory (`vendor/aosp/aosp`, 2026-09-27). sql.js builds are cached per patch and script under `~/.cache/webdroid` (`vendor/sql.js/rebuild.sh`).

### Reference AVD and gen-framework

- **Environment**. Use `JAVA_HOME=/opt/homebrew/opt/openjdk@21`; `scripts/android-sdk.ts` finds the SDK at `/opt/homebrew/share/android-commandlinetools`. Boot `webdroid_api35_ref` with `emulator -avd webdroid_api35_ref -no-window -no-snapshot -no-boot-anim -no-audio`.
- **What gen-framework pulls**. Boot jars (`BOOT_JAR_PATHS`), `/apex/com.android.{i18n,tzdata}/etc` into `vendor/aosp/apex/` (`PLATFORM_DATA`), Monet colors, OsConstants (`scripts/DumpOsConstants.java` under `app_process`) and the boot-image profile (`profman` as root). `--settings`, `--properties` and `--identity [url]` record only those parts.

### Cleanup

- **Cleanup policy**. Home starts a fresh session because class definitions survive a failed boot. Changes without Android evidence (dialog teardown, unconditional layout replay) were withdrawn; splitting follows ownership, not file size.
- **JS tier removal** (2026-10-13). The removal deleted the DEX-to-JS tier and its tools; `smoke`, `fwtest` and `jbench` run the Wasm sweeps, and `src/jvm/runtime.ts` stays as the native registry. Removing the cold interpreter (`7f6fc18f`) cut `bun run aot` from 49 to 21 s.

## Removed designs

On 2026-10-13 the DEX-to-JavaScript tier and the Wasm cold interpreter/slow runtime with compiled resume and deoptimization guards (`7f6fc18f`) were deleted; on 2026-10-11 process iframes were replaced by one Worker per app process.

### DEX-to-JS tier

- **Suspend only when waiting**. Async-by-default methods suspended the whole stack on common operations; synchronous-unless-waiting took Mihon scrolling 27 → ~44 fps at 4× throttle.
- **Throw cost is stack capture** (2026-09-30..10-01). A throw caught one frame up cost 2.9 µs, 2.2 µs of it a 64-frame `new Error()`; nested suspension promises made each capture walk the chain (200 vs 14 µs). Measure capture separately and skip it where handlers discard the exception.
- **Per-frame overhead**. TS copies of libcore collections beat it 1.4–2.5× (~2 ns per frame; V8 would not inline suspension scaffolding), the origin of measured intrinsics. A non-inlined prologue thread lookup cost 2.4–3.0 ms per Mihon tap (once credited to thread priority). Back-edge fuel checks cost 12–18% of tight loops; moving or batching them gained nothing. Rejected: per-site inline caches (noise), proving calls synchronous (error paths defeat it), frame pooling (1.6–2.0× slower than allocation).
- **Engine frame retention**. JavaScriptCore elides tail-position frames and Bun keeps ~10, so one extra frame per call hid callers from stack-class lookup.

### Persisted code and compile Workers

- **Key stores by content** (2026-10-02). Bundles naming the code store by URL deleted each other's chunks, so warm launches ran cold (FitNotes 125k → 2.6k cold calls once keyed by a `src/` hash).
- **Keep frame identity** (2026-10-01). Persisted bodies misattributed frames, breaking caller-sensitive APIs; persisted code must keep frame-to-method identity.
- **Save late, in idle slices**. Periodic whole-script rewrites stalled YouTube. Save after a method's first call (not its compile), clear pending entries only after storage succeeds, and encode in idle slices (`fb29e91c`: a ~245 ms long task became ~150 ms of slices). Persisting every profiled body added 44.6 MB unused.
- **Drop unclaimed code** (2026-10-03). ES-module records keep every compiled function for the realm's life (+49–65 MB); dropping bodies unclaimed 30 s after the last claim took Mihon 669 → 559 MB. A budget by source length, not heap, ran out after Mihon's second tap.
- **Compile Worker pacing** (2026-10-07). Imports after first frame were too late; unthrottled ones finalized inside taps; three in flight, 250 ms after input, kept taps clear. A Worker must install its message listener before awaiting imports, or it loses the first request.

### Cold interpreter and deoptimization

- **No cheaper first tier** (2026-10-05..13). `?cold=1` never sustained an app flow: cold FitNotes failed launch after 1,026,058 cold calls and 76,130 slow modules; a cold Calculator made 2.8M slow calls without a frame. Feeder took 14.4 s vs 3.15 s compiled on demand, and it needed a second platform build. Review: `research/wasm/cold-slowops-review.md` in Git history.
- **Deopt capture nearly doubles code** (2026-10-05). Instrumenting every activation grew platform C modules 97.45%; capture at all 1,817,225 poll sites 82.67%, cut to 54.40% (~87% of the rest executable code). An epoch-only guard (`de483bf6`) saved bytes but was never CPU-compared; byte savings alone do not show a runtime gain.
- **Count, then time**. Sharing adapters by operand role cut a 75-site probe from 75 modules/86,902 B to 5/9,396 B; per-PC modules, whole-method typing and register copies were counted, never shown cheaper.
- **Yields need the host deadline**. Loops of immediately fulfilled async natives run only microtasks; yields must check the host turn deadline.
- **`MethodInfo` is mutable** (2026-10-05). Host overlays can clear `MethodInfo.codeOff`, so decoded-body caches key on DEX/code-offset identity; eager preparation must skip unreachable PCs, or dormant missing classes raise linkage errors early.

### Process iframes

- **Shared page thread** (2026-10-01..10). microG's 2.2–3.4 s boot landed inside Mihon `lat` taps (253–342 vs 60–62 ms), and Calculator tap variance (61–176 ms) tracked microG's start.
- **Keep zygote overlap** (2026-10-02). In an iframe FitNotes drew at 1735–1802 vs 1596–1611 ms: its zygote no longer overlapped the download.
